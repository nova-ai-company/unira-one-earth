# UNIRA: One Humanity, One Future — Book plan

**Readers:** general readers (no economics background), plus policymakers and researchers who want the evidence. Every number in the book comes from the reproducible UNIRA research (V6.2–V7.2) and is sourced in the endnotes; nothing is invented.
**Voice:** clear, warm, honest. It shows what is possible *and* what could go wrong. Short chapters (5,000–7,000 words), each opening with a human scene and closing with "What the evidence says / What we still don't know".
**Length:** about 80,000 words in 4 parts and 16 chapters.

## Part I — Why change is possible
1. **Enough for everyone.** Today's world already produces enough food and energy for a decent life for all 8.1 billion people. The problem is distribution, waste and organisation, not scarcity. *(V6.5)*
2. **Where our working hours go.** 45 billion hours of French work, followed product by product: what serves needs and what serves selling. *(V6.2, V6.3)*
3. **The 30-hour week.** Why it is for families and time together, what it costs in output (20% more per hour), and how universal work closes the gap. *(V6.2–V6.3, rule 14)*
4. **What we learned from the past.** The 35-hour week, the Soviet shortage economy, the welfare state, the EU. What worked, what failed, and why UNIRA is different. *(evidence reviews)*

## Part II — How UNIRA works
5. **One Earth, many regions.** One world entity, regions with local autonomy, no commercial competition, allocation by needs × population. *(rules 3–5, 9)*
6. **The U: money that serves people.** A salary scale from 5,000 to 25,000 U, fixed prices, and the money cap that keeps everything stable. *(V6.4, rules 6–8, 10, 16–18)*
7. **A home for every family.** Prices of 100,000–500,000 U, at most two homes, zero-interest credit, and building 109 bn m² with low-carbon materials. *(V6.4, V7.0)*
8. **Health, education and care for all.** Free care, the 59-million health-worker gap, and why disarmament and healthier lives help. *(V6.6)*
9. **Security, not scarcity.** Why people stop hoarding when they feel safe, and why energy and travel must still be metered. *(V7.1)*

## Part III — Within Earth's limits
10. **A comfortable life inside the climate budget.** 40 GJ per person, 6–13% less energy than today, solar and wind at twice the record pace. *(V6.6)*
11. **Materials, land and food.** A 2.7× cut in materials, halved waste, diets that fit the planet. *(V6.6)*
12. **No more wars.** 27 million soldiers, 2.5% of world output and 5.5% of emissions freed for life. *(rule 19, V6.6, V6.8)*

## Part IV — Getting there
13. **Better than the best market world.** The matched-resources comparison: why needs-first allocation converges and markets do not. *(V6.8)*
14. **Guarding against power.** Errors, leakage and corruption, and the safeguard articles. *(V6.9, constitution Articles 33–39)*
15. **The transition.** Who joins first, what happens to property, savings and debts, and how a union of willing regions grows. *(V7.2)*
16. **One humanity, one future.** What remains uncertain, how to test it, and an invitation to criticise and improve.

**Appendices:** the 20 design rules; the draft constitution articles; data sources; how to reproduce every number.
