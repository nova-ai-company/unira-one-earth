# V9.1 — proposed constitution changes

**Status (2026-11-05): all three approved by the author on the research's recommendation and incorporated** as Article 4c, Article 10f.6 and T-9 of the Draft Constitution (with the drafting clarifications listed in its notes for the author, and the consistency check in `CONSTITUTION_CONSISTENCY_CHECK.md`). The original proposals follow for the record.

The author asked for changes only where the evidence supports them. Each proposal below states its evidence and how strong it is. Accept, change or reject each one separately.

## What the evidence does not justify changing

Much of what V9.1 tests is **already in the constitution**:
- transparent, rule-based allocation: Articles 26a and 33;
- independent audits: Article 36;
- an independent Court open to every person: Articles 32 and 32a;
- grievance procedures: Articles 35 and 10f.1;
- civilian mediation and early warning: Article 10f.4–5;
- protection against concentrated power: Articles 5a–5c, 22a, 30a, 39a and 42a;
- equal representation of regions: Articles 23–24.

V9.1 gives no reason to weaken or replace any of these. It does not show that they would work at world scale either. That can only be learned under Article 10e.6.

## Proposal 1 — No permanent exclusion from power within regions (new Article 4c, after Article 4b)

**Finding.**
- **Groups shut out of power rebel more often.** Groups excluded from central power, especially those recently downgraded, more often take up arms against the government (Cederman, Wimmer & Min 2010; association).
- **Including groups goes with less ethnic war.** Accommodation of groups (rights, autonomy, inclusion) plausibly explains the decline of ethnic civil war since the Cold War (Cederman, Gleditsch & Wucherpfennig 2017; association).
- **The current text has a gap.** It protects minorities' culture (Article 4a.3) and the equality of regions (Articles 23–24). It does not protect groups from permanent exclusion *within* a region.
- **Strength:** consistent observed associations; no experiment exists.

**Proposed text.**
> **Article 4c — No permanent exclusion.**
> 1. No community defined by ethnicity, religion, language or origin may be permanently excluded from political power or public office in its region, by law or in practice.
> 2. Each region chooses how it includes all its communities, according to its own traditions (Article 4): for example proportional representation, local autonomy, consultation rights on matters that especially affect a community, or shared executive office. One Earth does not impose a model.
> 3. Every person may still vote and stand for office as an individual. No one is required to declare a community membership, and no office is reserved for life to any community.
> 4. A community that has had no share in regional power for ten years may ask the Court to review whether point 1 is respected. The Court may order the region to propose a remedy; it may not impose one.

**Why the safeguards.** Power sharing can also fix ethnic divisions in place (point 3 avoids compulsory group membership). One Earth must not override regional autonomy (Articles 4 and 31), so the remedy stays with the region.

## Proposal 2 — Recovery after conflict (new point 6 in Article 10f)

**Finding.**
- **This is the strongest pattern in the panel.** A country with a conflict in the past 10 years has about **8 times** the odds of a new one.
- **Lower relapse is one of the two largest levers in the model.** Without it, conflict-years in scenario C rise by about 23%.
- **Supporting associations:** power sharing in settlements goes with more durable peace (Hartzell & Hoddie 2003). Institutions that constrain elites go with less repeat war (Walter 2015). Peacekeeping goes with more than 55% lower relapse risk (Fortna 2004).
- **A warning:** mediated agreements tend to be less durable later (Beardsley 2011), so the period after an agreement needs follow-up.
- **Strength:** strong observed pattern for the risk; associations, not experiments, for the remedies.

**Proposed text** (new point in Article 10f):
> 6. **After a conflict ends.** For at least ten years after an armed conflict ends in a region, One Earth gives that region priority support for a lasting peace, at the region's request and with its participation: implementation and follow-up of any agreement; inclusion of the parties and communities in public life (Article 4c); justice, reparation and the safe return of displaced people; and priority delivery of essential services (Article 10e). Any presence of the Peace Force remains governed by Articles 5a–5c. Progress is assessed every year under Article 10e.6.

## Proposal 3 — Paced reform where institutions are weak (new transitional provision T-9)

**Finding.**
- **Political change can raise the risk of violence.** Intermediate regimes and periods of political change are associated with more civil war (Hegre et al. 2001; Cederman, Hug & Krebs 2010).
- **Anti-corruption reform can upset existing arrangements.** Patronage can buy short-term peace (Fjelde 2009).
- **In the model,** if rapid reform in weak institutions doubled onset risk, the last 1% of people would reach essential security about 10 years later.
- **Reforms fail often:** one in three historical gains was reversed.
- **Imposed reform does not last:** community programmes imposed from outside did not change institutions (Casey, Glennerster & Miguel 2012).
- **Strength:** observed associations. The V9.1 panel itself is inconclusive on this risk.

**Proposed text.**
> **T-9. Institutional reform at a safe pace.**
> 1. Where institutions are weak or communities distrust central authority, the institutional changes of the transition are introduced in stages, agreed with the region and its communities, and adjusted when the monitoring of Article 10e.6 finds rising tension.
> 2. Pacing applies only to institutional changes. It never delays essential services (Article 10f.2) or reduces any right of Article 6.
> 3. Each stage is assessed publicly before the next one begins.

## Not proposed

- **No new body for representation or anti-corruption.** The existing bodies are already there; adding more would add concentrations of power.
- **No numeric targets** for institutional quality, mediation or relapse in the text. The evidence does not establish their size; targets belong in law and research (as in Article 10e.5).
- **No use of institutional indicators to rank, condition or sanction regions.** That would contradict Articles 3c.4, 10f.2 and 26a.2.

## Consistency notes (to be checked in full before adoption, as for V9.0)

- **Proposal 1** relies on Articles 4, 4a.3, 6 ("take part in decisions"), 31 and 32, and leaves regional autonomy intact.
- **Proposal 2** extends Article 10f, which the author approved in V9.0, and uses 10e.6 for assessment.
- **Proposal 3** follows T-7 and T-8. It is explicitly subordinate to Articles 6, 9 and 10f.2.
