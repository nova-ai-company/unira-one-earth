Consistency check of the constitution changes approved on 8 November 2026 (evening)

Decision (author, 2026-11-08, evening): "I agree with the three." (1) The proposal text of PROPOSED_CONSTITUTION_CHANGES_2026-11-08.md: the T-8.5 addition on how training is organised, and the new Article 18.12 on how homes are built. (2) Inside it: a household's own building hours are both deducted from the home's price and counted as work under Article 7, as drafted; regions may allow, not require, participation. (3) The three earlier open points: Article 4c and T-9 join the protected core; weapons of mass destruction follow the T-5 conditions; the T-5.2 thresholds stay a research item. The third point was approved as a group, so the drafting of each is listed below for the author to reverse if the reading was wrong.

The changes, as incorporated in v75/constitution/UNIRA_CONSTITUTION_DRAFT.md

  • T-8.5 (addition): training for the professions in shortage is organised so that no building limits it: theory online where shown to be as effective; schools, laboratories and simulation centres used for practice in rotation every day; every hospital, clinic and school a place of training; trainees move between hospitals and regions with housing and travel provided; hours of supervised practice never reduced; children's schooling in person.
  • Article 18.12, How homes are built: repair, extend and convert first; a decent core for every household without one, designed to be extended; then extension to the comfortable standard; factory parts and the materials of each region (bamboo, earth, timber, low-clinker cement; clinker and steel only where height or ground demand them); factories located under Article 10a.5 in the regions of need; industrial regions supply machines, training and materials in shortage; a household may build part of its own extension, its hours deducted from the price and counted as work; no core left unextended past the regional deadline. A research-basis note follows the article.
  • Article 42a.1.11: the ban on permanent exclusion of communities within regions (Article 4c) and the rule that paced reform never delays rights, essential services, safeguards or elections (T-9.2) join the protected core.
  • T-5.6, Weapons of mass destruction: held weapons are placed under independent international verification from the joining day; never tested, increased or transferred; no new ones made; destroyed under Article 5.5 within five years of the day the member's T-5.2 conditions are met; neither One Earth nor the Peace Force ever holds them.

Drafting choices made on incorporation (reversible)

  1. Protected core, T-9: only T-9.2 (the guarantee that pacing never delays rights, services, safeguards or elections) is protected, not the whole of T-9, because T-9.1 and T-9.3 are procedure that a future amendment may reasonably improve.
  2. T-5.6, what is frozen: testing, increase and transfer are forbidden from day one and no new weapons may be made. Maintenance for safety is not forbidden, so that a frozen arsenal does not become more dangerous while it waits.
  3. T-5.6, who holds them: the weapons stay under the member's civilian authority during the wait; the text says expressly that One Earth and the Peace Force never hold them, so that Article 5.5 ("never rebuilt") is not undermined by a transfer.
  4. Article 18.12, "may build": participation is a right of the household, never a duty; a region may organise it, not impose it. This is the reading of the author's agreement with the draft's "allows and does not require".
  5. Article 18.12, double credit: the hours are deducted from the price (Article 13, embodied hours already paid in kind) and count as work under Article 7 for the duration. They are not paid a second time in salary: the text says "count as work", which satisfies the duty to contribute, not "are paid".

Open points for the author

  1. Whether self-build hours should also be paid at the base salary for the hours worked (they would then be both an income and a price deduction). The incorporated text does not pay them; it deducts them and counts them toward the duty to contribute.
  2. Protected core and Article 4c's remedy clause: protecting 4c also protects 4c.4's limits on the Court (proposed remedy only). If the author wants the Court to be able to do more later, 4c.4 should be excluded from the core.
  3. Frozen weapons and exit: a member that leaves One Earth before its T-5.2 conditions are met takes its frozen arsenal with it. Whether verification should survive exit is a treaty question, not settled here.
  4. The T-5.2 thresholds (federation size, Peace Force strength) remain a research item (review question 13).
  5. The book draft (v73/book/) still describes the earlier rules; to be revised as a whole.

Conclusion: no inconsistency was found. The reading of "the three" as approval of each listed point is recorded above so that any part can be reversed.
