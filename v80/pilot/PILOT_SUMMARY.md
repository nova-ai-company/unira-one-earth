# A 30-hour, full-employment town: a pilot proposal (one page)

**For:** mayors, intercommunalities, foundations. **Full plan:** `PILOT_PLAN.md`. **Sources:** `evidence.csv`.

## The idea
For four years, a town of 10,000–100,000 people tries six things together, all inside today's French law:
1. **A job for everyone who wants one.** Permanent contract, 30 hours, at least full-time minimum wage.
2. **A 30-hour week at full pay** in employers that volunteer.
3. **An open register.** Every euro, job and home the pilot allocates is published within 30 days.
4. **Social homes by points plus a public lottery**, with checks on claims and a must-live-in rule.
5. **A citizens' assembly drawn by lot.** The council must answer every recommendation in writing.
6. **Strong safeguards:** random audits, community monitoring, and protection for whistleblowers.

An opt-in local voucher with a holding cap is a small, seventh test.

## Why it is credible
| Precedent | Result |
|---|---|
| Territoires zéro chômeur (France, 83 territories) | 4,051 jobs; net public cost €11,300–13,700 per job per year; few move on to ordinary jobs (evaluation committee) |
| Marienthal job guarantee (Austria, 2020–24) | Long-term unemployment nearly eliminated; better wellbeing; no harm to other workers. Randomised and independently evaluated |
| Iceland, UK, Portugal shorter weeks | Output held in most workplaces; 92% of UK firms kept it; only 4 of 41 Portuguese firms went back |
| ProZorro open procurement (Ukraine) | Up to 30% savings with more bidders; thousands of suspicious cases flagged by citizens |

## What we will learn
Each prediction is filed publicly **before** the start. Each failure is published.

| Question | Success | Failure |
|---|---|---|
| Does employment move toward 88%? | +10 points vs similar towns | under +5 points |
| Do people take the jobs? | ≥ 61% of unemployed in work within a year | ≤ 41% (no better than today) |
| Does output per hour rise at 30 h? | recovers ≥ 20% of lost hours | under 10% |
| Do funds reach their target? | ≤ 8% unaccounted | > 15% two years running |
| Are homes allocated fairly? | richer applicants get no advantage | they get > 10% more offers |
| Does the assembly matter? | ≥ 50% of proposals implemented | < 25% |

## Cost and time
- **Core version** (300 guaranteed jobs, town of 20,000): about **€45 m gross over 5.5 years, €26–29 m net** of benefits saved and taxes paid.
- **Full version** (800 jobs): about **€100 m gross, €54–61 m net**.
- **Timeline:** preparation 2027; pilot 2028–2031; final public evaluation 2032.

## What we ask of a town
A council vote supported by the opposition. Access to anonymised local data. An independent university evaluator. A share of the funding, or help to raise it from the State, the European Social Fund+ and foundations.

## Honest limits
This pilot cannot test world-wide allocation, fixed prices or compulsory work. It tests whether UNIRA's local building blocks behave as UNIRA predicts. If they do not, the project will say so.
