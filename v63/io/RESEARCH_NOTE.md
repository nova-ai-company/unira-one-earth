# UNIRA V6.3 (part 1) — Where France's working hours go, and how much is avoidable

**Status:** accounting in **hours of human work**. It is not a forecast, and it is not a model of UNIRA's currency. Base year 2022 (latest complete French input-output table); 2019 as pre-COVID check. All numbers are generated by `run.py` → `outputs/`.

## 1. Principle: the current economy is the starting point, not the model
UNIRA's currency (U) and economic institutions differ from today's market economy (user clarification, 2026-11-03). This study uses the current economy for one purpose only: to measure the **matched physical starting point** that every scenario must share (master spec, A/B/C counterfactuals):
- what is produced;
- which products are needed to make which;
- how many hours of work each requires.

The official symmetric input-output table (`naio_10_cp1700`) is in euros. Here it serves only as a *technical proxy for physical interdependence*: the share of inputs in each product's production. **No market price, profit, wage or money flow enters any result. Every result is in hours.** Euro-based coefficients still carry price distortions (e.g. high-margin services look more input-intensive). Later versions should replace them with physical units (energy, materials, food mass).

## 2. Method (`src/unira_io/model.py`)
- **Domestic technical coefficients:** A = Z_dom / x, for 65 products that partition total output exactly (check).
- **Direct hours per unit:** l = national-accounts hours (all employed persons) / output.
- **Total hours multipliers:** m′ = l′(I−A)⁻¹, solved exactly in pure Python. Inverse residual is below 10⁻⁹ (test).
- **Hours embodied in a final use f:** m′f. The six final uses sum to total hours to within 4·10⁻⁷ (check).
- **Hypothetical extraction:** the hours saved if a product disappeared as final and intermediate good. **Partial reduction:** the same with a share r. Both are upper bounds, because users of the product are assumed to need no substitute.
- **Hours allocation (disclosed):** France does not publish hours for every A64 division. 51% of hours (e.g. retail inside total trade, each financial sub-sector) are split from the published parent total in proportion to published **persons employed**, i.e. equal hours per person within a group. Example of the bias: retail has more part-time work than wholesale, so retail hours are overstated.
- **Product vs industry:** hours are by industry and the table is product-by-product. The model assumes one industry per product.
- **Imports:** the table is domestic-only. "Import hours-equivalent" is the hours France would need to produce its imports with its own technology. It indicates labour embodied abroad; it does not estimate foreign hours.

## 3. Where France's 45.0 bn hours go (2022)
| final use | bn hours | share | 2019 share |
|---|---|---|---|
| household consumption | 14.42 | 32.0% | 33.9% |
| government services (health, education, administration…) | 11.55 | 25.6% | 25.0% |
| non-profit institutions | 1.54 | 3.4% | 3.4% |
| investment (buildings, equipment, infrastructure) | 7.74 | 17.2% | 17.8% |
| exports | 9.69 | 21.5% | 19.5% |
| imports, hours-equivalent at French technology | **10.88** | (24% of domestic hours) | |

**Reading.**
- **Only a third of French work time serves household purchases.** A quarter delivers public services that UNIRA guarantees, and is not avoidable. A sixth builds the capital stock.
- **More than a fifth serves other countries.** For a world federation those exports meet other people's needs, so they cannot simply be dropped.
- **France receives more labour than it sends:** about 10.9 bn hours-equivalent in, 9.7 bn out.

The largest household-consumption chains are:
- retail trade, 3.0 bn h;
- restaurants and accommodation, 2.4 bn h;
- food manufacturing, 1.3 bn h;
- insurance, 0.66 bn h;
- residential care, 0.60 bn h;
- health care, 0.57 bn h.

## 4. Avoidable output
### 4.1 Market-specific activities (normative candidates)
Seven activities are flagged as candidates. Six exist mainly because of market institutions UNIRA replaces:
- advertising and market research;
- real-estate services excluding imputed rent;
- financial services;
- financial auxiliaries (brokerage, fund management);
- insurance;
- legal, accounting, head-office and consulting work.

Employment services were examined and **excluded**: temp workers are recorded there but work in client industries, so removing agencies does not remove their work. **Choosing the candidates and their reduction levels is a democratic, normative decision.** The levels below illustrate it; they are not evidence.

| reduction level | hours freed (incl. supply chains) | share of all hours | 2019 |
|---|---|---|---|
| modest (5–25%) | 0.77 bn | 1.7% | 1.6% |
| moderate (15–50%) | 1.75 bn | 3.9% | 3.7% |
| deep (30–75%) | 2.97 bn | 6.6% | 6.3% |

### 4.2 Longer-lasting durable goods (Article 11, stock-first)
Domestic hours in household and investment purchases of durables (electronics, equipment, vehicles, furniture, textiles) are only about **0.4 bn (0.9%)**. Lifetime extensions of +25/+50/+100%, net of extra repair (0.25 h per hour saved, SYNTHETIC), save 0.14/0.23/0.35% of French hours. **Imported durables embody about 1.27 bn hours-equivalent, three times more.** Stock-first mainly saves labour, materials and emissions **abroad**. That is a real benefit for a world federation, but it does little to close France's own 30 h gap.

### 4.3 Food
Food chains for household consumption (agriculture, fishing, food manufacturing) account for 3.6% of hours. Waste reduction can save only a fraction of that, and this study has not quantified it (evidence task: Eurostat food-waste statistics by stage).

## 5. Closing the 30 h gap from year 1 (decision: 30 h from the start)
These are the hours still uncovered after V6.2's evidence pathway (behavioural offset θ plus labour mobilisation), as a share of total hours. Each column then subtracts market-candidate reductions:

| V6.2 case | uncovered | after modest | after moderate | after deep |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| low | 13.5% | 11.8% | 9.6% | 6.9% |
| **central** | **10.1%** | 8.4% | **6.2%** | 3.5% |
| high | 1.5% | closed | closed | closed |

**Findings**
1. **Market-specific activities cannot alone close the 30 h gap.** Even deep reductions (6.6% of hours) leave 3.5% uncovered in the central case and 6.9% in the low case.
2. **UNIRA's distinctive institutions do help.** A moderate reduction in market-specific activity covers about 40% of the central residual. This is the first quantified, UNIRA-specific advantage in the programme, and it is attributable to institutions rather than to an assumed efficiency bonus. It still has to be tested against a reformed-market baseline (B), which could also shrink some of these activities.
3. **The rest must come from somewhere else in year 1:**
   - higher mobilisation take-up (being estimated empirically now);
   - higher θ;
   - other output choices (e.g. less export production, more local repair);
   - or a temporary, explicitly managed shortfall that protects essential needs first.
4. **Public services (25.6% of hours) and investment (17.2%) are not candidates.** They deliver UNIRA's guarantees and its future capacity.

## 6. Limitations
- Euro-based coefficients proxy physical structure. Price differences distort input intensities.
- 51% of hours are allocated by persons within parent groups.
- Upper-bound extraction: no substitute inputs, and no new activity created (e.g. UNIRA's own payment and allocation system, UGTN, needs hours too; not yet counted).
- IO year 2022 is combined with V6.2's 2023 hours gap as a share. The two years differ.
- Product-industry assumption; import technology assumption.
- Candidate levels are normative illustrations, not estimates.
