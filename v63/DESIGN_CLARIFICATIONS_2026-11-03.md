# UNIRA design clarifications from the user (2026-11-03)

These are additions to `handover/01_PROJECT_MASTER_SPECIFICATION.md`. The original file is unmodified.

| # | clarification | consequence for the research |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **30 h week from the start**; no staged 35 → 30 h path. | Feasibility is tested in year 1 (`v63/ledger`). The 35 h results are a comparator only. |
| 2 | **UNIRA's currency and economy are not the current market model.** | Current-economy data is used only as the matched physical starting point. Results are in hours and physical units, never euros. No U–euro conversion. |
| 3 | **The world is one entity; today's countries become regions.** | Country studies are regional pilots. Labour and production are pooled across regions by locating production where people are available, not by forced relocation (spec rule). Exports and imports become inter-regional transfers. |
| 4 | **No commercial competition for profit.** Constructive comparison of ideas and innovation remains. | Consistent with the spec's "constructive competition of ideas". Market-specific activities (advertising, speculative finance, etc.) are candidates for reduction (`v63/io`). |
| 5 | **Equity: all resources are divided by needs and by the number of people in each area.** | The decisive test becomes world-level: can world production provide a comfortable standard for everyone on a needs × population basis? Rich regions (e.g. France) may receive fewer resources per person than today. This must be measured, not assumed away. |
| 6 | **The 5,000–25,000 U salary range is intended.** | Pay differs up to 5×, while *resources* are allocated by need. Phase 3 must show how the two co-exist: purchasing power, essential goods guaranteed outside pay. |
| 7 | **"Extra goes to the One Earth entity."** Recorded interpretation: regional surplus beyond needs (production, resources, distributable enterprise surplus) goes to the global entity for needs-based redistribution. | The monetary model needs a One Earth fund with inflows from regional surplus. Not yet confirmed: whether pay above the 25,000 U ceiling is also covered. |
| 8 | **Fixed prices for essentials (food, health, …): no price change, even after 1,000 years.** | Prices of essentials become a constitutional constant. The burden of balance moves entirely to *physical* supply, buffer stocks and income rules. The model must test whether nominal demand at fixed prices ever exceeds physical supply (shortage or queue risk), especially under shocks. |
| 9 | **The One Earth entity decides which region produces what**, and everything is sent to regions according to their number of people and their needs. | Production location and distribution are planned centrally in physical terms. The model needs a world allocation module: supply by region, needs by region, transport, and buffer stocks. To check: compatibility with the spec's regional/local autonomy (subsidiarity) and with "no forced relocation" (production must go where people are). |
| 10 | **Money circuit, the user's example:** 1 kg of tomatoes = 1 U (fixed). Base salary 5,000 U/month; experience multipliers 1.2×, 1.3× … up to 5× (25,000 U). A business owner's profit counts as salary, capped at 5×. Anything above goes to the region, and from the region to the One Earth entity. That surplus finances people who cannot earn a salary (5,000 U health/disability guarantee, child benefit, …). | Essentials are **bought with salaries at fixed prices**, not handed out separately. The model must enforce one balance condition per region and period: total U income (wages + capped owner salaries + guarantees + child benefits) minus saving = total value of goods available at fixed prices. Surplus flows business → region → One Earth must cover all guarantees. |
| 11 | **Housing:** buying, building or renovating a home costs between **100,000 and 500,000 U** (fixed maximum price by floor area, location, etc.). Each adult may own **at most 2 homes**, and each family **at most 2 homes in total**. Homes are comfortable but not castles or very large. | The model needs a fixed housing price schedule (area × location), ownership caps, affordability in years of salary, a financing rule (savings and/or loans; open), allocation of desirable locations at fixed prices (excess demand), and guaranteed housing for people who cannot buy (spec: safe housing for all). |
| 12 | **Salary multipliers (1× to 5×) rise with experience, knowledge/education and the complexity of the work.** | The model needs a points system mapping experience, education and job complexity to a multiplier. To keep fixed prices stable, the overall distribution must stay matched to output (experience creep is tested). |
| 13 | **Unspent U:** savings are allowed and kept. Each region also keeps **fixed, studied catastrophe reserves** of goods. Principle: **no waste, no overproduction**. | Savings are household deposits with a matching liability in the U system. Reserve stocks are sized from observed shock frequencies, then rotated (consumed and replaced before spoilage). Production = consumption + reserve replacement + investment, nothing more. |
| 14 | **Most important: everyone (able to work) has to work, to produce.** The 30 h week exists **to keep families together and give them time together**. | Labour mobilisation is a core design rule, not an optional policy. The year-1 ledger gets "universal work" scenarios (20–64 employment rate → 84 / 88 / 92%). The 30 h rule's purpose is social (family time), which the welfare evaluation must count. Rights check: a work *obligation* must stay compatible with the ILO Forced Labour Conventions (C029, C105). Free choice of occupation, exemptions for illness, care and study, and the 5,000 U guarantee for those unable to work are what keep it compatible. |
| 15 | **Retirees:** income until death, set **case by case by need** (not a flat 5,000 U). It covers food, daily life and **one holiday a year**, and is lower if they own their home. Couples receive an amount suited to two. **Health care and education are free.** | Retiree income = needs basket at fixed prices × household equivalence (couple vs single) + housing cost if not owner. Financed by regional surplus. Health and education are produced by public services and paid from the regional fund, not by households. |
| 16 | **Home purchase:** households save **at least 50%** of salary, so a home takes about 3 years for one earner, faster with two. **Zero-interest credit** is available, repaid directly from salary. | Saving ≥50% halves consumer spending, which constrains the fixed price level. Credit creates U with a matching liability, and repayment extinguishes it (stock-flow consistency). The model tests whether housing demand at fixed prices fits construction capacity, and what happens to savings once homes are owned. |
| 17 | **Saving is personal, up to 50%.** 50% is the *maximum* for someone living modestly but comfortably (food, one holiday a year, clothes twice a year). People who spend more save less. | Saving propensity varies by person (0–50%). The average is lower than 50%. Modelled as a distribution in the lifecycle simulation. |
| 18 | **Money cap.** Personal money is capped. Amounts above the cap, including large inheritances, go to the One Earth entity. The cap is set **every year by a coefficient** that keeps people working to produce the needed resources. If technology and AI replace work, **working hours are reduced** instead. The aim is resources for the whole Earth. | Implemented as an annual cap rule (cap = coefficient × annual base salary). The coefficient is adjusted so total deposits stay within what goods and reserves can back. Excess goes to One Earth, as does inheritance above the cap. Productivity gains become shorter hours (rule 13 "no overproduction"). Tested for: no money overhang, work incentive (share of people at the cap), home access. |
| 19 | **No wars** (disarmament), and **far less disease**: in UNIRA, better lifestyles, no stress and no fear of the future should prevent much illness. | Military personnel, industry and energy are freed for useful work, e.g. as a recruitment pool for the health-worker gap. Health-demand reduction is a **hypothesis tested with evidence**: attributable fractions of disease to modifiable risks, working hours, job insecurity and poverty. Its size and *timing* (benefits arrive over decades) are estimated, not assumed. Countervailing effects are counted: longer lives mean more elderly care. **Addition: no energy is used to build or run weapons.** Military energy use (≈5.5% of global emissions incl. supply chains; Parkinson & Cottrell 2022, needs recheck) is freed; in V6.7 this lets rich regions cut energy 3%/yr instead of 4%/yr within the climate envelope. |
| 20 | **Security reduces over-consumption (hypothesis):** when people know they can always afford and access food and essentials, they stop over-buying, hoarding and over-eating. Analogy: once mobile calls became unlimited for a fixed price, nobody called 24 hours a day. | Tested as a hypothesis, not assumed. Expected to hold for goods with natural satiation (food calories, calls, basic goods) and to fail for goods without it (energy, travel, data, status goods), where cheap or unlimited access tends to raise use (rebound). UNIRA keeps fixed prices rather than zero prices, which limits rebound. Effects enter the model as evidence-based reductions in waste and precautionary buying. **Tested (V7.1): holds for food, calls, basic goods and hoarding; fails for energy, travel and data (rebound 10–30%). Proposed: energy, travel and data stay metered and priced per unit, never flat-rate.** |

## Scientific challenges these create (to be tested, not assumed)
- **Global sufficiency.** World output per person must be at least the comfortable basket per person, plus investment and ecological limits. This needs a global multi-regional input-output model (OECD ICIO / EXIOBASE) and a defined basket.
- **Coordination without prices.** Physical planning in hours and materials, which V6.3 starts, has to handle billions of products and preferences. Information and latency are falsification test 5.
- **Voluntary participation.** Moving production to where labour is available takes capital, infrastructure and time; moving people must stay voluntary.
- **Legitimacy.** Regions that receive less than today must consent democratically to the allocation rule.

## Specific risks of rules 8–9 (to be tested, with historical evidence)
- **Fixed prices without physical balance cause shortages, not stability.** Economies that froze staple prices for decades (e.g. the Soviet Union; analysed in Kornai, *Economics of Shortage*, 1980) saw queues, rationing, informal markets and rising subsidies. The fixed price held; availability did not. UNIRA's answer must be physical: production planned to needs, buffer stocks, and incomes that cannot exceed the value of available essentials at the fixed price.
- **1,000 years of shocks.** Harvests, climate, epidemics and resource depletion vary supply. Fixed prices then require strategic reserves and inter-regional transfers sized to observed shock frequencies (testable with FAO yield-variability data).
- **Productivity and new products.** With prices fixed and wages fixed, productivity gains must become either shorter hours or more goods. New products need a price-setting rule.
- **Central coordination at world scale.** Deciding production for about 8 bn people and millions of products is the "knowledge problem" (falsification test 5). Modern input-output planning and computing make parts of it tractable. Preferences, quality and local conditions still need regional information and autonomy.

## Proposals arising from V6.4 — provisionally accepted (user replied "go" after they were proposed, 2026-11-03; revisable)
- **P1. Inherited homes:** a home inherited beyond the heir's first home returns to the regional pool, or the heir chooses which one to keep. Reason: in simulation, second homes come mainly from inheritance while renters wait 15–19 years.
- **P2. Low money cap + zero-interest credit:** cap about 1–1.5 years of base salary, recalculated yearly; homes financed by credit repaid from salary. Reason: a cap above the home price cannot stabilise money.
- **P3. Guaranteed public housing** for adults without a salary.
