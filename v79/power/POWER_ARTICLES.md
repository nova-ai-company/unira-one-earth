# One Earth constitution — power articles (draft for Draft 2)

*V7.9, 3 November 2026. These are new and amended articles that put the author's design direction A–G and the fixes from `STRESS_TEST.md` into constitutional text. They use new numbers that slot in without renumbering the 48 existing articles. Each article says which existing article it follows or amends.*

*Each clause carries a mark. **[E n]** means it is directly supported by evidence, where n is a row of `evidence.csv` (row 1 is the first row after the header). **[J]** means it is a design judgement: reasoned, but not directly tested by evidence. Most of the evidence comes from nation states (see `evidence.md`). Notes in italics are not part of the legal text.*

---

## Part I additions

**Article 3c — Models that cannot be secretly changed.** *(Follows Article 3b. Strengthens 3b.3 and 3b.6.)*
1. Every model used by One Earth runs only in a published, signed version. The Audit Authority rebuilds it each year from the public code and data before it is used, and checks that the running version is identical. [J]
2. Every forecast used for an allocation plan comes from at least two models built by independent teams. If they disagree by more than their stated uncertainty, people decide by the manual procedure of Article 3b.6. [J]
3. Each year the Scientific Council and outside experts from several regions try to manipulate the models and their data. Their report is public. [J]
4. No model may score persons, rank their loyalty or behaviour, or select persons for sanctions, policing or audit. Audits of persons are chosen at random or by a published rule. [E 45, E 46]
5. No model may use personal payment data. Models use aggregated data only (Article 12a). [J]
6. Each decision names the person who adopted it and the model version used. [J]
7. A model found to be manipulated is withdrawn at once. The manual procedure applies, and every decision taken with it is reviewed. [J]

*Basis: the Dutch childcare-benefits risk model used nationality as a risk factor, which led to discrimination (E 46). The EU AI Act bans social scoring (E 45). Rules for model integrity are judgement; there is no evidence base yet at this scale.*

**Amendments to Article 5a — The Peace Force.**
- **Replace 5a.2 with:**
  1. To enforce a ruling of the Court, the Force may be deployed with that ruling and approval by double majority of the Council of Regions.
  2. Every other deployment needs a majority of the Assembly of the Peoples and a double majority of the Council of Regions (Article 25). The Court reviews it within 7 days and may halt it.
  3. Under an immediate threat to life, the Command Board (Article 5b) may act for at most 72 hours. It may act only at the request of a region's government or of the Court, and it informs the Court at once. Then it needs the approvals above. The 72 hours cannot be renewed.

  [J; E 24 for the risk of executive emergency action]
- **In 5a.3, replace "commanders rotate every 3 years" with:** commanders serve one term of at most 3 years at each level of command and never return to the Command Board. [J]
- **Add 5a.1, second sentence:** "It is never used against peaceful citizens, against an election or a referendum, or against a region's vote to leave One Earth (Article 41a)." [J, protected by Article 42a]

**Article 5b — Arms under many keys.** *(Follows Article 5a.)*
1. **No single commander.** The Force is led by a Command Board of five members from five continents, who decide by majority. There is no commander-in-chief. [J]
2. **Mixed units.** Every unit of more than 100 members has members from at least ten regions on at least three continents. No region provides more than 10% of a unit. [J, informed by E 9, E 10]
3. **No commander over their own people.** No commander serves in their own region. No commander leads a unit in which people from their own region exceed 5%. [J, informed by E 9]
4. **Heavy weapons dispersed.** Heavy weapons are kept in depots in many regions, and no depot holds more than 5% of any category. Heavy weapons are armour, artillery, combat aircraft, armed drones, warships and missiles. [J]
5. **Many keys.** A heavy weapon is released only when three keyholders act together, each from a different region:
   - one from the Force;
   - one appointed by the Inspector-General;
   - one appointed by the Council of Regions.

   Weapons carry technical locks, so they cannot be used without these keys. [E 40, E 41]
6. **No other armed body.** No body of One Earth or of a region may create any other armed force, guard or intelligence service with arms. The Protection Service is unarmed. [E 8, E 12]
7. **Records.** Every key use, every movement of heavy weapons and every deployment is logged. The Inspector-General and the Audit Authority check the logs. Summaries are published each year. [J]

*Basis: under nuclear two-person rules and permissive action links, no single person can arm a weapon (E 40, E 41). The coup literature shows that loyalty-based coup-proofing weakens forces (E 8). Ethnic stacking or purging provokes rebellion and coups (E 9, E 10). Counterbalancing defeats coups mainly because some soldiers resist (E 11). Creating new forces raises coup risk (E 12). A force mixed by rule, not by loyalty, applies these findings; the exact percentages are judgement.*

**Article 5c — Lawful orders only.** *(Follows Article 5b.)*
1. Every member of the Force must refuse an order that clearly breaks this Constitution, above all an order to act against peaceful citizens, an election, the Court or a region's vote to leave. [E 11, E 29]
2. No one may be punished, dismissed or disadvantaged for refusing such an order, or for reporting it. [E 29]
3. A member who refuses may go directly to the Inspector-General and the Court. The Court rules within 7 days. [J]
4. Obeying an unlawful order is no defence. [J]
5. Every member learns these duties in training and swears to this Constitution, not to any person. [J]

*Basis: the German Basic Law gives a right to resist anyone abolishing the constitutional order (E 29). Coups fail when some soldiers refuse (E 11).*

## Part II additions

**Article 6a — Free information.** *(Follows Article 6. Gives detail to "expression" in Article 6.)*
1. Everyone may seek, receive and share information and ideas. There is no prior censorship. [E 5, E 6]
2. No body of One Earth or of a region may shut down, slow or filter networks or platforms for a group, a region or a topic. [E 5, E 6]
3. A court may order specific content removed, case by case, only for an offence defined by law. No other body may do so. [J]
4. Digital networks use open standards. In each region they are run by several independent operators, and no operator carries more than a third of the traffic. [J]
5. No body of One Earth may own, fund or control news media, except the public media of point 6. [J]
6. Public media are governed by boards from many regions, chosen partly by lot and partly by journalists. No office-holder appoints them. Their funding is fixed by rule. [J]
7. No person, enterprise or body controls more than a quarter of the news audience in any region. [J]
8. Journalists' sources are protected. [J]

*Basis: media censorship is the most common tactic of autocratizing governments, used in 73% of the 44 autocratizers (E 5), and freedom of expression is "often the first domino to fall" (E 6). The structural limits (a third, a quarter) are judgement.*

**Article 9a — Emergencies.** *(Follows Article 9.)*
1. **Who declares.** The Assembly of the Peoples (by majority) and the Council of Regions (by a majority of regions) declare a state of emergency, on a proposal of the Coordination Council. If they cannot meet within 72 hours, the Coordination Council may declare one for at most 72 hours. [J]
2. **How long.** An emergency lasts at most 30 days. Each renewal, for at most 30 days, needs two-thirds of the Assembly and two-thirds of regions. Without renewal it ends by itself. [E 25]
3. **Where.** It applies only to the regions and matters affected. [J]
4. **Court review.** The Court reviews every declaration and renewal within 7 days. It may end them or any measure under them. [E 24, E 22]
5. **Never.** No emergency may:
   - suspend or delay an election or a referendum;
   - dissolve or suspend the Assembly, the Council of Regions, the Court, a regional government or any body of Article 22a;
   - reduce the rights of Article 6 (Article 9);
   - change this Constitution or any law by decree;
   - freeze accounts or open personal data without a court order (Article 12a);
   - censor information (Article 6a);
   - use the Force against peaceful people.

   [E 24, E 25, E 18]
6. **Publish.** Every emergency measure is published within 24 hours, with its reasons and end date. All measures end with the emergency. [J]
7. **No amendments.** This Constitution may not be amended during an emergency or within 6 months after it. [J]
8. **Brake.** More than 90 days of emergency in any year triggers the brakes of Article 39a. [J]

*Basis: democracies are 75% more likely to erode under a state of emergency (E 24). Only 55% of national emergency clauses set a time limit, and nearly 40% allow decrees with force of law (E 25). Delaying elections is a common and usually successful evasion (E 18). The 30-day limit and supermajority renewal come from the author's direction E; the exact numbers are judgement.*

## Part III additions

**Article 12a — Your money and your data are yours.** *(Follows Article 12. Gives detail to 12.3 and to Article 37's "as the law allows". Amends 12.2 as in point 5.)*
1. **No freeze without a judge.** An account may be frozen, seized or restricted only by the order of a court, for a named person, a stated offence and a limited time. The person is told and may appeal. No administrative body, emergency or model may do it. [E 27]
2. **Needs are never blocked.** Even under a court order, every person can always pay for food, home, health, energy and transport at the level of Article 6. [J, Article 9]
3. **No data without a judge.** Personal payment data are opened only by a court order about a named person. Every access is logged. The person is told after the investigation ends, and at the latest after one year. The number of accesses is published each year. [E 27]
4. **Anonymous everyday payments.**
   - Everyday payments by a person are anonymous to everyone except the payer, up to a limit per payment and per month.
   - The starting limits are 200 U per payment and 2,000 U per month. The Monetary Authority adjusts them by published rule.
   - The limits are never lowered in the 12 months before an election.

   [E 37, E 39 for feasibility; limits J]
5. **Invoices name the seller, not the buyer.** For anonymous payments, the invoice of Article 12.2 names the seller and the goods, not the buyer. [E 39]
6. **Offline.** Payments work offline, from person to person, with cash-like privacy. *(Superseded by the author's rule 38, 2026-11-03: UNIRA has no cash and only online payments. The constitution's 12a.6 now guarantees essential needs during network failures instead.)* [E 37]
7. **Where data live.** Personal payment data stay in the region where the person lives. They are held on open-source, distributed systems run by at least three independent operators and audited every year. Bodies of One Earth receive only aggregated data, about groups of at least 1,000 people. [J]
8. **Deletion.** Personal transaction details are deleted after 2 years, unless a court order covers them. Enterprise accounts are kept as the law requires. [J]
9. **No scoring.** Payment data are never used to score, rank or punish people (Article 3c). [E 45]

*Basis: the ECB's digital euro design gives cash-like privacy offline and stops the issuer linking online payments to users (E 37). BIS Project Tourbillon shows cash-like payer anonymity is technically feasible (E 39). In Canada in 2022, accounts were frozen without a judge under emergency powers, and a court later found this violated the right against unreasonable search and seizure (E 27).*

**Article 17b — The Monetary Authority: rules, not discretion.** *(Follows Article 17a. Amends Article 17.1: "set each year by a published coefficient" becomes "set each year by the Monetary Authority under Article 17b".)*
1. The Monetary Authority has five members from five continents. The Court appoints them from a public shortlist drawn up by the Scientific Council. They serve one term of 7 years. [J; selection by Court appointment under direction A]
2. Its only tasks are:
   - to keep the U working;
   - to apply the fixed hour price of Article 13;
   - to compute the money cap of Article 17 each year by the published thermostat rule;
   - to set the anonymous-payment limits of Article 12a by rule.

   [E 35, E 36]
3. It has no discretion. It may not:
   - set prices;
   - allocate resources;
   - lend;
   - create U except by published rule;
   - treat any person, enterprise or region differently from others.

   [E 36]
4. **The thermostat rule.** The cap moves each year by a published formula, within at most 10% up or down. The formula is based on the measured stock of unspent money compared with its target and on hours worked compared with need. Anyone can recompute the result from public data. [J; the formula itself is still a research item, see Article 17 notes]
5. **One cap for everyone.** The same cap applies to every person in every region. [J]
6. **Changing the rule.** The rule changes only by the procedure of Article 26a.3. A change never takes effect in the 12 months before an Assembly election. [J]
7. The Audit Authority checks each year's figure. If a figure differs from what the rule gives, the rule's figure applies, and the Court enforces it. [J]
8. The Authority holds no personal payment data (Article 12a). [J]

*Basis: political control of money is associated with worse outcomes (E 35), and committed rules beat discretion (E 36). These findings come from inflation-targeting central banks. The U has fixed prices, so the risk here is political use of the cap, not inflation. The evidence transfers by analogy only.*

## Part IV additions

**Article 22a — No body holds arms, money and allocation together.** *(Follows Article 22. Adds "armed force", "money" and "science" to the powers that Article 22 separates.)*
1. These powers are held by separate bodies:

   | Power | Body |
   |---|---|
   | Law | Assembly of the Peoples and Council of Regions |
   | Executive | Coordination Council |
   | Allocation | Allocation Authority (Article 26a) |
   | Money | Monetary Authority (Article 17b) |
   | Armed force | Peace Force (Articles 5a–5c) |
   | Measuring needs | Statistical Office |
   | Auditing | Audit Authority |
   | Science | Scientific Council |
   | Judging | Court |

   [J, direction A]
2. No person is a member of two of these bodies. [J]
3. No person moves from one of them to another within 3 years. [J]
4. The bodies are chosen by different methods, and no body chooses the members of a body that audits or judges it:

   | Body | How it is chosen |
   |---|---|
   | Assembly of the Peoples | Elected directly (Article 23) |
   | Council of Regions | Chosen by the regions (Article 24) |
   | Coordination Council | Elected by the Assembly (Article 27) |
   | Allocation Authority | Elected by the Council of Regions by double majority |
   | Monetary Authority | Appointed by the Court from a Scientific Council shortlist |
   | Inspector-General of the Force | Appointed by the Court |
   | Command Board of the Force | Elected by the Council of Regions by double majority, from a list checked by the Inspector-General |
   | Statistical Office head | Elected by two-thirds of the Assembly from a Scientific Council shortlist; one 7-year term |
   | Audit Authority board | Drawn by lot from auditors nominated by regional audit bodies, then confirmed by the Assembly |
   | Scientific Council | Drawn by lot from scientists nominated by regional academies, with every region and discipline represented |
   | Citizens' assemblies | Drawn by lot (Article 32b) |

   [J; E 31–E 34 for lot]
5. Each body's budget is a share fixed by law, which can be cut only by the procedure of Article 42b. The Court's and the Audit Authority's budgets are fixed in this Constitution. [J]

**Article 26a — Allocation without favour.** *(Follows Article 26. Replaces 26.3 and limits Article 38.)*
1. The Allocation Authority has nine members from different continents, elected for one 5-year term (Article 22a). [J]
2. **Needs only.** The formula of Article 10 depends only on measured needs and population. No allocation may depend on how a region or its people vote, on their politics or opinions, or on loyalty to any body. [J, Article 2]
3. **Changing the formula** needs:
   - two-thirds of the Assembly;
   - a double majority of the Council of Regions;
   - a published opinion of the Scientific Council;
   - review by a citizens' assembly (Article 32b).

   A change takes effect only after the next Assembly election. [J]
4. **Formula against actual.** Each year the Register (Article 33) shows, for every region, what the formula gives and what was allocated. A region whose gap exceeds 2% receives reasons within 30 days. Any region or citizen may ask the Court to correct it. [E: Article 33 basis, Reinikka & Svensson in the v75 draft; 2% is J]
5. **Discretionary allocations** are at most 2% of the yearly total and follow a published rule. Loss of priority under Article 38 needs a ruling of the Court. [J]
6. **Honest statistics.** The Statistical Office publishes its raw data and code. The Audit Authority and any region may replicate its figures. A difference of more than 5% goes to the Court. [J]
7. No region's allocation is cut as a sanction except by ruling of the Court. It never falls below what Article 6 requires. [J, Article 9]

**Article 30a — Limits on office.** *(Follows Article 30. Adds to 30.1–30.3.)*
1. **Terms.** No person holds a One Earth office for more than 10 years in a lifetime. Executive, allocation, monetary, command and judicial offices are held for one term only. [E 15–E 20; durations J]
2. **No benefit to oneself.** A change to the length of terms, the number of terms or the pay of an office never applies to anyone holding that office, or elected to it, when the change is adopted. [E 16, E 20]
3. **Pay.** Office-holders are paid within the Article 14 scale, from 1 to 5 times the base salary. They receive no other income and no gifts above a small value fixed by law. [J, direction D]
4. **Assets.** Office-holders declare their assets, and those of their household, each year while in office and for 5 years after. The Audit Authority checks the declarations and they are published. [J]
5. **After office.** For 5 years after leaving office, no one may:
   - lobby the body they served in;
   - work for a region or enterprise they decided about.

   Judges and members of the Monetary Authority and the Command Board hold no other public office or paid post for 5 years after their term. [J]
6. **No families in power.** No close relative of an office-holder serves in the same body at the same time. [J]
7. **Elections on time.** Elections to the Assembly and to regional parliaments are held on fixed dates and are never delayed. If a disaster makes voting impossible in a region, that region votes within 90 days, and no other region's vote is delayed. [E 18]

**Article 32a — An independent Court.** *(Follows Article 32. Adds to 32.1.)*
1. The Court has 21 judges, at most one from any region and at least three from each continent. This number is fixed by this Constitution. [E 19, E 22; number J]
2. One-third of the judges are replaced every 4 years, so that no single majority appoints the whole Court. [J]
3. The judicial commission of Article 32.1 has three equal parts:
   - judges of regional high courts, drawn by lot;
   - members nominated by regional bar associations and law faculties;
   - citizens drawn by lot.

   No office-holder sits on it. [J]
4. A judge may be removed only for grave misconduct or incapacity, by two-thirds of the other judges and two-thirds of the Council of Regions. [J]
5. The Court's budget is a fixed share of One Earth's administrative budget, set in this Constitution. Judges' pay is never reduced during their term. [J]
6. No law may remove a matter from the Court's jurisdiction. Every body obeys its rulings. [E 22, E 23]
7. The Court rules within 7 days on emergencies, deployments, account freezes and exit disputes. [J]

*Basis: established independent courts prevent slides to authoritarianism, but new courts are fragile (E 22, E 23). Courts were used in 30% of successful term-limit overstays (E 19). So the Court's independence must be built into its structure, not trusted.*

**Article 32b — Citizens' assemblies by lot.** *(Follows Article 32a.)*
1. **Citizens' Council.**
   - A permanent Citizens' Council of 100 people is drawn by lot each year. It is balanced by region, sex, age and education.
   - It may place items on the agenda of the Assembly, and the Assembly must debate them within 6 months.

   [E 32]
2. **Review of major decisions.** A citizens' assembly of at least 500 people, drawn by lot in the same way, reviews:
   - every change to the allocation formula, the thermostat rule or this Constitution;
   - every emergency renewal beyond 60 days;
   - every Force deployment beyond 90 days;
   - every trigger of Article 39a.

   [E 31, E 33, E 34; scope J]
3. If two-thirds of the citizens' assembly oppose a decision, the decision is suspended. It takes effect only if the Assembly then confirms it by two-thirds. [J]
4. Members receive full pay while they serve. They are protected against any pressure, and they serve once in a lifetime. [J]

*Basis: lot-based assemblies are now common (289 cases catalogued by the OECD, E 31). They have been made permanent in places such as Ostbelgien (E 32). In Ireland they opened a decision that politicians had avoided (E 33, E 34). There is no evidence yet that they stop capture at world scale. Their role here as a brake is judgement.*

## Part V additions

**Article 39a — Democracy health check and automatic brakes.** *(Follows Article 39.)*
1. **The Democracy Monitor.**
   - Each year an independent Democracy Monitor publishes indicators, with their uncertainty, for One Earth and for each region.
   - The Monitor is a panel of research institutes from at least five continents, chosen by lot from qualified applicants.
   - It is paid from a fixed share and does not depend on any office-holder.

   [E 1–E 6 for the method; J for the structure]
2. The indicators cover at least:
   - elections that are free, fair and on time;
   - freedom of the media and of expression;
   - independence of the Court and whether its rulings are obeyed;
   - freedom of association and civil society;
   - account freezes and data accesses (counts);
   - emergency days;
   - Force deployments and complaints;
   - leakage and the formula gap (Article 26a.4);
   - how concentrated offices are among persons and groups.

   [J]
3. Methods are public. A change of method needs the Scientific Council's approval, and past years are recalculated with it. [J]
4. **Brakes.** If the overall index, or the indicator for elections, media or the Court, falls by more than its uncertainty for two years running, these follow automatically:
   1. extra audits of every body within 6 months;
   2. a citizens' assembly (Article 32b), which reports on causes and remedies; the Assembly votes on its proposals within 6 months;
   3. all emergency powers end, and no new ones may be declared for 12 months, except for disaster relief that restricts no rights;
   4. early Assembly elections within 6 months, unless the citizens' assembly finds by two-thirds that the fall came from causes outside the control of One Earth's bodies.

   [J, direction F]
5. A fall in one region triggers extra audits and a citizens' assembly in that region. It never allows One Earth to dissolve the regional government (Article 31.2). [J]
6. No body may suspend or delay these brakes. [J]

*Basis: V-Dem shows that democratic decline can be measured each year, with uncertainty, by indicator (E 1–E 6). That freedom of expression falls first (E 6) is why media is a trigger indicator. No existing constitution has automatic brakes like these, so the mechanism is untested judgement.*

## Part VI additions

**Article 41a — Leaving One Earth.** *(Follows Article 41. Gives the procedure for its first sentence.)*
1. **Request.** A region asks to leave by:
   - a decision of its parliament by majority; or
   - a citizens' initiative signed by 10% of its voters.

   [J]
2. **Referendum.** The region holds a referendum within 2 years of the request. It is supervised by the Court and by observers from other regions. [E 42 (Ethiopia: within 3 years); J]
3. **Threshold.** The region leaves if a majority of votes cast says so, with turnout of at least 50% of voters. No higher threshold may be set after the request. [J; E 44 shows how a 2/3 threshold kept a 61.7% majority in]
4. **Default date.** Exit takes effect 2 years after the vote, or earlier or later if both sides agree. One Earth cannot block it by refusing to agree. [E 43]
5. **Fair share.**
   - Assets built in the region stay with it.
   - The region receives its population share of the common reserves and of the One Earth Fund's liquid assets, minus commitments still due.
   - If there is no agreement within 18 months, the Court decides by a published formula.

   [J; E 42 (Ethiopia's "division of assets")]
6. **No force, no punishment.** Until exit, allocations and Article 6 rights continue in full. The Peace Force leaves the region before exit, with its heavy weapons. Force, threats of force, or cutting allocations against a region that asks or votes to leave are forbidden. [J, protected by Article 42a]
7. **People first.** Every person in a leaving region may keep residence or move to a member region. People belonging to minorities keep the protection of Article 4a until the new arrangements protect them as well. [J]
8. **No endless votes.** If a vote to leave fails, no new request may be made for 5 years, unless two-thirds of the regional parliament ask for one. [J]

*Basis: Article 50 TEU shows that a default exit date stops the centre from stalling (E 43). Nevis shows that a supermajority set by the centre can override a clear majority (E 44). Ethiopia's Article 39 provides a full procedure with a deadline and asset division (E 42). Whether exit rights reduce or increase conflict is NOT established by the evidence gathered here. In Ethiopia a written exit right did not prevent war (not verified here). Exit is protected here because, with only one armed force, it is the last check on capture.*

**Article 42a — The protected core.** *(Follows Article 42. Extends 42.2.)*
1. No amendment may abolish or weaken:
   1. the rights of Article 6, Article 9 and Article 4a.2;
   2. free, secret, universal elections on fixed dates, at least every 5 years, for the Assembly and every regional parliament;
   3. term limits (Articles 27, 30 and 30a);
   4. the separation of powers and the independence of the Court (Articles 22, 22a, 32 and 32a);
   5. the ceilings of the Peace Force, and the ban on using it against peaceful citizens, elections or a region's vote to leave (Articles 5a.1, 5a.4, 5b and 5c);
   6. the court-order protection of money and data (Article 12a.1–3);
   7. the limits on emergencies (Article 9a);
   8. the right of persons and regions to leave (Articles 4b.2, 41 and 41a);
   9. the equality of regions, with no permanent seats or vetoes (Article 2);
   10. this Article and Articles 42 and 42b.

   [E 28, E 30]
2. Amendments may strengthen these guarantees. [J]
3. Before any referendum on an amendment, the Court rules on whether it touches the core. An amendment that does is void. [E 30 (Roznai); J]

**Article 42b — Safe amendment.** *(Follows Article 42a. Adds to 42.1.)*
1. Besides the majorities of Article 42.1, the world referendum must be won by a majority of voters and in a majority of regions. [J]
2. An amendment to Parts IV, V or VI needs two votes of the Assembly, separated by an Assembly election. [J; E 16, E 20]
3. A citizens' assembly reviews every amendment (Article 32b). [J]
4. No amendment may be made during an emergency or within 6 months after one. No amendment may benefit anyone holding office when it is adopted (Article 30a.2). [J; E 16]

*Basis: two-thirds of term-limit evasions use amendment (E 16, E 20), and courts often helped (E 19). Unamendable cores are now in 53% of new constitutions (E 30) and follow the German model (E 28). There is little evidence on whether eternity clauses actually stop backsliding, as opposed to being common. So the core is paired with Court review, citizens' review and slow two-step votes.*

## Transitional addition

**T-7. Safeguards before power.** *(Follows T-6. Strengthens Article 5a.5.)*
1. The Court, the Audit Authority, the Inspector-General, the Democracy Monitor and the Citizens' Council are in place before the first Force unit is formed, before the U is issued and before the first allocation. [E 12, E 23]
2. In its first 5 years the Force does not exceed 250,000 members. [J]
3. National armies are dissolved by the same rules of mixing, so no group is stacked into the Force or purged from it. Every former soldier is offered work, training and full income (Article 5.3). [E 9, E 10]

**Count of clause marks.** Out of 111 marks, 34 are evidence-supported ([E]), 18 are judgement informed by evidence ([J; E…]) and 59 are pure judgement ([J]). This is expected: the evidence tells us *which doors* power uses (amendment, emergencies, courts, media, money, arms). It rarely tells us the exact number, such as 30 days, 2% or 21 judges, that keeps a door shut.
