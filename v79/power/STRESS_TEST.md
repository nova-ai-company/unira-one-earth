# One Earth constitution: power stress test (V7.9)

*Red-team of the v75 draft constitution (`v75/constitution/UNIRA_CONSTITUTION_DRAFT.md`, 48 articles) against attempts to capture power. "E#" refers to a row of `evidence.csv` (row 1 is the first row after the header). A–G refer to the design direction the author approved. New article numbers are drafted in `POWER_ARTICLES.md`.*

## Why this matters

One Earth would hold the only armed force, the only currency and the power to allocate. Today's evidence says the main danger is not a sudden coup. It is a gradual, legal-looking erosion by people who were elected. Open coups and blatant fraud are declining while "executive aggrandizement" is rising (Bermeo 2016, E14). 74% of humanity lives in autocracies and 44 countries are autocratizing (V-Dem 2026, E1–E4). In those 44 countries, media censorship is the most common tactic, used by 73% of them (E5). At least a third of presidents who reached their term limit since 2000 tried to stay, two-thirds of those attempts went through constitutional amendment, and courts helped in 30% of successful overstays (E15–E19). Democracies are 75% more likely to erode under a state of emergency (E24). The draft must hold up against all of these.

## Verdict

**The draft's strengths.** Its institutional core is stronger than most national constitutions on four points:
- size bias: square-root seats, a 5% cap and the double majority;
- Peace Force composition: a 5% regional cap, rotation and service outside one's own region;
- an absolute size and budget ceiling for the Force;
- transparency: the register, random audits and open models.

Several of these choices are directly backed by evidence on leakage and on coup-proofing by mixing, not by loyalty (E9–E11).

**The draft's weaknesses.** It is weakest exactly where modern backsliding happens. The evidence says it happens through law, amendment, emergencies, courts, money and information (E14–E19, E24, E5). Five gaps are serious enough that a determined majority could use them to capture One Earth legally:

1. **No emergency article** (scenario 6).
2. **No court-order requirement for freezing accounts or reading payment data** in a cashless system (scenario 5).
3. **The amendment rule and core democratic guarantees are not entrenched**, so the eternity protection covers only Art. 6 and Art. 9 (scenarios 1 and 10).
4. **The Court is not structurally protected**: its size, budget, jurisdiction and removal are open (scenario 8).
5. **Exit has no procedure or timetable**, and the regions are disarmed (scenario 9).

Next come undefined bodies with real power: who sets the money cap, who chooses the Allocation and Statistical bodies, and the executive's direct control of Force deployment. Last come the missing media article and the missing integrity checks on models.

**After the fixes.** With the articles in `POWER_ARTICLES.md`, every scenario meets at least two independent barriers: a rule, and a different body chosen by a different method that can enforce it. Most scenarios also meet a third: automatic brakes and the right to leave.

**What no text can guarantee.** The fixes do not make capture impossible. A constitution's median life is about 19 years (E21). Term-limit evasion is stopped more often by popular resistance than by courts (E17). The design therefore leans on dispersal, so that many people in many regions must cooperate for any abuse to work, and on exit, so that capture can be escaped. It does not lean on any single guardian.

**Residual risks that remain after the fixes.**
- Collusion across all bodies at once, for example after an ideological landslide in which a single movement wins every election and lottery pool over a decade.
- A world-scale information environment the Union does not control.
- The transition period (T-5), when a new Force is built and national armies are dissolved. Both steps are associated with higher coup risk (E10, E12).
- A union that loses legitimacy so broadly that exit becomes a cascade.
