UNIRA research V8.1 · Constitution Article 13

What will things cost?

In UNIRA, a price is the hours of work a product needs, all inputs included, times a price of one hour fixed forever (146.39 U). Goods that use scarce natural resources carry a resource surcharge. Prices never change afterwards; productivity gains shorten working hours instead.

Energy for needs is cheap

Every person has an essential energy allowance for home electricity, heating, cooking and essential travel, set from measured needs. Energy prices rise in three steps. Step 1, within the allowance: only the work it needs, a kilowatt-hour of electricity about 0.17 U (seconds of work), a litre of fuel about 0.8 U. Step 2, up to 1.5 times the allowance: half the resource surcharge. Step 3, beyond that: the full surcharge, because cheap energy beyond needs is used more and would break the climate limit. Energy used to grow and transport food is never surcharged, so food stays cheap. If the allowance covers 80% of what households use today, the household basket becomes only 1.53% dearer, instead of 6.12% with the surcharge on everything.

Each surcharge is the smallest that keeps the good at least as dear, compared with other goods, as it is today. Without it, fuels would become about 80% cheaper and electricity about 65% cheaper, encouraging waste. The surcharges raise the cost of the household basket by 6.12%; their revenue goes to the One Earth Fund. Homes are priced separately (Article 18).
