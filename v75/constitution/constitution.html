The Constitution of One Earth (UNIRA) — Draft 1

Draft for discussion, 3 November 2026. Written for the author of UNIRA from the design rules, research results (V6.2–V7.4) and evidence reviews. Notes in italics give the evidence or reasoning behind an article; they are not part of the legal text.

---

Preamble

We, the people of the Earth, one humanity in many regions, cultures and languages, establish this Constitution:

  • to end war;
  • to share the Earth's resources fairly, by need and by the number of people in each place;
  • to give every person a dignified and comfortable life and time to live it;
  • to protect the planet for those who come after us.

Fairness is the core of this Constitution. No region shall rule another. No person or group shall own power over the common good. Every decision shall be based on data and science, made in the open, and answerable to the people. We produce what we need, for all regions.

---

Part I — Foundations

Article 1 — One Earth. The Earth is one federation, called One Earth (UNIRA). Its members are regions: today's countries, or parts or unions of them, as their people decide.

Article 2 — Equality of regions and people.

  1. Every region has equal dignity, and every person has equal rights.
  2. No region, alliance of regions or institution may hold power over another region except through this Constitution.
  3. There are no permanent seats, no vetoes, and no privileges of size, wealth or history.

Article 3 — Fairness first, and four principles. Fairness is the core of every decision. All public action is guided by:

  • Evidence: decisions use data and science, published and open to challenge (Article 3a);
  • Equity: resources go first to unmet needs;
  • Justice: rights are equal and enforceable;
  • Sharing: what the Earth provides belongs to all, and its use is accounted for openly.

Article 3a — Data and science in every decision.

  1. Every decision of One Earth and of the regions on production, allocation, prices, salaries, the money cap, the environment and public services is based on measured data and the best available science.
  2. The data, the methods and the reasons for each decision are published, with their uncertainty, so that anyone can check them.
  3. An independent Scientific Council, drawn from all regions and all relevant sciences, reviews the methods used. Its opinions are public. A decision that departs from its advice must say why.
  4. When new evidence shows that a decision or rule is wrong, it is reviewed. Rules carry their own tests of success and failure (as in Article 39).
  5. We produce what we need, for all regions:
    • production is planned from measured needs and population in every region, plus the studied reserves of Article 11;
    • no more is produced than is needed, and no region is left without what it needs.

Article 3b — Artificial intelligence serves the decision; people decide.

  1. By default, One Earth uses open data models, including artificial intelligence and machine learning, to forecast the needs of every region, to propose production plans and their locations, and to check the data for errors and diversion.
  2. Tested before use, and while in use. A model is used only if it predicts better than simple published rules (such as "same as last year" or "the recent trend") on data it has not seen. Its accuracy is re-tested every year against what really happened, and the results are published.
  3. Open. The code, the data, the training method and the accuracy of every model are public. Anyone may rerun them. No secret model may decide anything.
  4. Fair. Every model is tested for each region and each income group. A model that is systematically less accurate, or biased, for any region may not be used for that region until the gap is corrected. Better statistics in the regions with the weakest data come first.
  5. People decide and are accountable. A model gives a proposal, its uncertainty and its reasons. The responsible Council decides, may depart from the proposal and must then say why. Any region or citizen may challenge a decision before the Court of One Earth.
  6. Limits. No model may decide on a person's rights, on sanctions against a person or region, or on the use of force. Models use aggregated data, and personal data only as the law allows (Article 37). A manual procedure always exists, so that decisions continue if a model fails.
  7. Reserves (Article 11) are sized from the measured forecast errors, so that every region is covered even when a forecast is wrong.

Basis: V7.8 (v78/RESEARCH_NOTE.md).

Article 3c — Models that cannot be secretly changed. (Follows Article 3b. Strengthens 3b.3 and 3b.6.)

  1. Every model used by One Earth runs only in a published, signed version. The Audit Authority rebuilds it each year from the public code and data before it is used, and checks that the running version is identical.
  2. Every forecast used for an allocation plan comes from at least two models built by independent teams. If they disagree by more than their stated uncertainty, people decide by the manual procedure of Article 3b.6.
  3. Each year the Scientific Council and outside experts from several regions try to manipulate the models and their data. Their report is public.
  4. No model may score persons, rank their loyalty or behaviour, or select persons for sanctions, policing or audit. Audits of persons are chosen at random or by a published rule.
  5. No model may use personal payment data. Models use aggregated data only (Article 12a).
  6. Each decision names the person who adopted it and the model version used.
  7. A model found to be manipulated is withdrawn at once. The manual procedure applies, and every decision taken with it is reviewed.

Basis: the Dutch childcare-benefits risk model used nationality as a risk factor, which led to discrimination (v79/power evidence row 46). The EU AI Act bans social scoring (v79/power evidence row 45). Rules for model integrity are judgement; there is no evidence base yet at this scale.

Article 3d — Innovation and improvement.

  1. Research and development are a duty of One Earth. At least 3% of all working hours go to research and development, and at least a third of that to developing and scaling up new products and methods, not only to research.
  2. Open results. Results paid for by One Earth or a region are published openly, with their data. Inventors are named and honoured. No one may own an idea so as to stop others from using it.
  3. Many funders, fair choice. Research money is given by at least three independent bodies. Proposals are first checked by experts for soundness. Among those that pass, money is given partly by lottery, so that new and unusual ideas are not shut out. At least a fifth goes to high-risk work or to people funded for the first time.
  4. Prizes and purchase promises. For a clearly defined problem, One Earth may offer a prize or a promise to buy the solution. The rules and judges are fixed in advance and may not be changed against those who have already competed.
  5. Whoever improves, gains. A team that reduces the hours needed to make a product keeps part of the hours saved for a limited time, as set by law. A unit's targets are never raised because it did better than planned. The gain is given to the team as:
    • shorter working hours at the same salary;
    • a step up the salary scale for its members, within Article 14;
    • resources for the team's next project (workers, materials, equipment), which belong to the work, not to any person;
    • public recognition.
  6. Never above five times. No prize, gain or reward under this Article raises anyone's monthly pay above five times the base salary (Article 14), or their savings above the money cap and protected accounts (Article 17). A personal prize in U is at most one year of the winner's salary. Larger prizes go to the team's next project or to the One Earth Fund, with full public credit to the winners.
  7. Freedom to try, duty to stop. Any group may propose a project or co-operative. It needs approval from one of several independent bodies, not all of them. Every project has a budget, an end date and a published test of success. A project that fails its test is closed. The people who tried keep their salary level and may try again.
  8. People choose. For goods in common use, people can choose between designs from different teams whenever this is practical. Teams whose designs are chosen are publicly recognised. Production follows the choices people make.
  9. Fair and visible rewards. The criteria for salary multipliers are published, and everyone may see why they are paid what they are paid and may appeal.
  10. Measured every year. The Scientific Council (Article 3a) publishes, for One Earth and for each region:
    • (a) the share of working hours in research and development;
    • (b) the hours of work needed to produce the basket of essential needs, and how fast this falls;
    • (c) the share of production from products or methods introduced in the last 5 years;
    • (d) the share of research money going to new applicants and to high-risk work;
    • (e) user satisfaction with the quality and choice of common goods;
    • (f) the number of new projects and co-operatives started per 100,000 working people, and how many survive 5 years;
    • (g) how fairly workers judge their pay, and whether they feel free to propose improvements;
    • (h) the delay between a new technology being proven anywhere in the world and its general use in One Earth.
  11. Automatic review and reform.
    • If, for 2 years in a row, research and development falls below 2.5% of working hours, or the hours needed for the essential basket fall by less than 0.5% a year, or the measure in 9(d) falls below 15%, the Assembly must review this Article and the salary rules within one year.
    • If, for 5 years in a row, the hours needed for the essential basket fall by less than 0.5% a year, or the measures in 9(c), 9(e) or 9(h) are worse than in the 5 years before, the rules on prices, salaries and research funding must be reformed by law. Any measure that fixes the failure is allowed, including new rewards for innovators.
  12. Defence does not starve civilian invention. The share of research hours spent on defence is published every year and limited by law.

Evidence: V7.9 (v79/innovation/evidence.md): public research underlies all 210 new drugs approved in the USA in 2010–16; open data raise follow-on research; unexplained pay differences cut effort. A fixed price per hour would penalise teams that save hours, so clause 5 lets them keep part of the saving. Thresholds are provisional until measured starting values exist.

Article 4 — Subsidiarity and diversity.

  1. Decisions are taken as close to the people as possible.
  2. Each region governs itself according to its own religion, culture, language and traditions:
    • education;
    • local planning;
    • public holidays;
    • daily life.
  3. One Earth decides only what must be decided together:
    • shared resources;
    • allocation between regions;
    • the currency;
    • rights;
    • the environment;
    • peace.

Article 4a — Culture, belief and the individual.

  1. A region may give a religion, culture or language an official or traditional place in its public life.
  2. Every person keeps freedom of thought, conscience and belief:
    • no one may be forced into a religion or belief;
    • no one may be punished for their religion or belief;
    • no one may be punished for changing their religion or belief, or for having none.
  3. Minorities in every region are protected in their language, culture and worship.

Article 4b — Borders and movement.

  1. Each region controls its own borders. It decides who may enter, stay, work and settle, by its own visa and residence rules, applied equally and without discrimination by origin, religion or ethnicity.
  2. Every person may leave any region, including their own, and return to their own region.
  3. Protection in emergencies. A person whose life is threatened by persecution or disaster is protected:
    • first in their own region, through rebuilding and needs-first allocation by One Earth (Article 10);
    • where that is impossible, other regions provide temporary shelter, shared among regions by their means and population through the One Earth Fund;
    • people return home when it is safe again.

Evidence (v76/migration/): all 117.8 million people forcibly displaced at end-2025 fled persecution, conflict or violence, which this Constitution abolishes; 14.7 million returned home in 2025. Disasters displaced 45.8 million people in 2024, 99% weather-related, almost all inside their own country, which is why protection starts in the person's own region. Economic migration (281 million migrants in 2020) falls as living standards converge but does not vanish, and may rise temporarily while poorer regions catch up; regional visa rules manage it.

Article 5 — Peace.

  1. War is abolished.
  2. No region may maintain armed forces or weapons of war, or produce them. All arms belong to One Earth.
  3. Existing armed forces are dissolved during the transition. Their members are offered work, training and full income, including in health, construction and civil protection, or service in the One Earth Peace Force.
  4. Disputes between regions are settled by the Court of One Earth (Article 32), whose rulings bind.
  5. Weapons of mass destruction (nuclear, chemical, biological) are destroyed under independent verification and never rebuilt.

Evidence: military spending is 2.5% of world output (SIPRI Fact Sheet, April 2026, on 2025 data), military activity about 5.5% of global emissions (SGR/CEOBS 2022), and armed forces number 27.4 million people (World Bank). UN peacekeeping uses about 59,000 uniformed personnel (peak 106,595 in 2015) on USD 5.6 bn a year, and peacekeepers are found to reduce violence against civilians. A Peace Force of about 500,000 frees about 26.9 million people and 2.45% of world output (v76/peace_force/). New armed forces are most at risk of coups while being built (De Bruin 2018), hence point 5 of Article 5a.

Article 5a — The One Earth Peace Force: the only armed force, under strict control. One Earth holds the only armed force so that the Court's rulings and people's rights can be enforced everywhere. Because whoever controls the only armed force could dominate everyone, it is bound by these rules:

  1. Purpose:
    • enforcing rulings of the Court of One Earth;
    • protecting people whose Article 6 rights are under attack;
    • responding to disasters together with the Protection Service.

It is never used against peaceful citizens or protest, a lawful election or referendum, a region's democratic institutions, or a region's vote to leave One Earth (Article 41a).

  1. Deployment:
    • to enforce a ruling of the Court, the Force may be deployed with that ruling and approval by double majority of the Council of Regions;
    • every other deployment needs a majority of the Assembly of the Peoples and a double majority of the Council of Regions (Article 25); the Court reviews it within 7 days and may halt it;
    • under an immediate threat to life, the Command Board (Article 5b) may act for at most 72 hours, only at the request of a region's government or of the Court, and informs the Court at once; the 72 hours cannot be renewed.
  2. No region controls it:
    • members come from all regions, with no region providing more than 5% of personnel or commanders;
    • commanders serve one term of at most 3 years at each level of command and never return to the Command Board;
    • members do not serve in their own region.
  3. Limited size:
    • the Force may never exceed 1,000,000 members (about 12 per 100,000 people);
    • its budget may never exceed 0.15% of world output;
    • within these ceilings its size is fixed by law (planned: about 500,000 members, about 0.05% of world output);
    • size, budget and every deployment are published each year in the Public Allocation Register.
  4. Build-up under watch: the Force is built up gradually during the transition, with members from all regions from the first day and the Inspector-General in place before the first unit is formed.
  5. Independent oversight: an Inspector-General of the Force, appointed by the Court, reports publicly. Every person harmed by the Force may bring a case before the Court.
  6. Regional police: regions keep civilian police for everyday crime, trained to One Earth human-rights standards and equipped only for policing.

Article 5b — Arms under many keys. (Follows Article 5a.)

  1. No single commander. The Force is led by a Command Board of five members from five continents, who decide by majority. There is no commander-in-chief.
  2. Mixed units. Every unit of more than 100 members has members from at least ten regions on at least three continents. No region provides more than 10% of a unit.
  3. No commander over their own people. No commander serves in their own region. No commander leads a unit in which people from their own region exceed 5%.
  4. Heavy weapons dispersed. Heavy weapons are kept in depots in many regions, and no depot holds more than 5% of any category. Heavy weapons are armour, artillery, combat aircraft, armed drones, warships and missiles.
  5. Many keys. A heavy weapon is released only when three keyholders act together, each from a different region:
  6. one from the Force;
  7. one appointed by the Inspector-General;
  8. one appointed by the Council of Regions.

Weapons carry technical locks, so they cannot be used without these keys.

  1. No other armed body. No body of One Earth or of a region may create any other armed force, guard or intelligence service with arms. The Protection Service is unarmed.
  2. Records. Every key use, every movement of heavy weapons and every deployment is logged. The Inspector-General and the Audit Authority check the logs. Summaries are published each year.

Basis: under nuclear two-person rules and permissive action links, no single person can arm a weapon (v79/power evidence rows 40, 41). The coup literature shows that loyalty-based coup-proofing weakens forces (v79/power evidence row 8). Ethnic stacking or purging provokes rebellion and coups (v79/power evidence rows 9, 10). Counterbalancing defeats coups mainly because some soldiers resist (v79/power evidence row 11). Creating new forces raises coup risk (v79/power evidence row 12). A force mixed by rule, not by loyalty, applies these findings; the exact percentages are judgement.

Article 5c — Lawful orders only. (Follows Article 5b.)

  1. Every member of the Force must refuse an order that clearly breaks this Constitution, above all an order to act against peaceful citizens, an election, the Court or a region's vote to leave.
  2. No one may be punished, dismissed or disadvantaged for refusing such an order, or for reporting it.
  3. A member who refuses may go directly to the Inspector-General and the Court. The Court rules within 7 days.
  4. Obeying an unlawful order is no defence.
  5. Every member learns these duties in training and swears to this Constitution, not to any person.

Basis: the German Basic Law gives a right to resist anyone abolishing the constitutional order (v79/power evidence row 29). Coups fail when some soldiers refuse (v79/power evidence row 11).

Part II — Rights

Article 6 — Guaranteed rights. Every person has the right to:

  • food and clean water, sufficient and secure, with access guaranteed;
  • a safe, comfortable home of a decent size for their household, never seized and never subject to forced relocation;
  • health care and education, free of charge, throughout life;
  • an income:
    • by work, from the base salary of 5,000 U a month;
    • if unable to work for health reasons, a guaranteed 5,000 U;
    • in retirement, a needs-based income for life covering food, daily life and one holiday a year;
    • for children, the allowance of Article 15;
  • time: a standard working week of 30 hours (6 hours × 5 days), so that families can live together;
  • energy, transport and digital access sufficient for a comfortable life;
  • privacy, freedom of thought, expression, belief and association;
  • movement within their own region, the right to leave any region and return to their own (entry to other regions follows each region's visa rules, Article 4b);
  • take part in decisions, to vote, to stand for office, to petition and to protest peacefully.

Article 6a — Free information. (Follows Article 6. Gives detail to "expression" in Article 6.)

  1. Everyone may seek, receive and share information and ideas. There is no prior censorship.
  2. No body of One Earth or of a region may shut down, slow or filter networks or platforms for a group, a region or a topic.
  3. A court may order specific content removed, case by case, only for an offence defined by law. No other body may do so.
  4. Digital networks use open standards. In each region they are run by several independent operators, and no operator carries more than a third of the traffic.
  5. No body of One Earth may own, fund or control news media, except the public media of point 6.
  6. Public media are governed by boards from many regions, chosen partly by lot and partly by journalists. No office-holder appoints them. Their funding is fixed by rule.
  7. No person, enterprise or body controls more than a quarter of the news audience in any region.
  8. Journalists' sources are protected.

Basis: media censorship is the most common tactic of autocratizing governments, used in 73% of the 44 autocratizers (v79/power evidence row 5), and freedom of expression is "often the first domino to fall" (v79/power evidence row 6). The structural limits (a third, a quarter) are judgement.

Article 7 — Work.

  1. Every adult able to work has the right and the duty to contribute through work, with free choice of occupation.
  2. Illness, disability, caring duties, study and retirement exempt a person from this duty without loss of income or rights.
  3. No one may be forced into a particular job.

This keeps the duty to work compatible with the international ban on forced labour (ILO Conventions 29 and 105).

Article 8 — Justice.

  1. Justice is independent, public and equal for all.
  2. It aims first at prevention and repair, then at rehabilitation.
  3. Punishment is proportionate and humane.
  4. The death penalty, torture and collective punishment are forbidden.
  5. Rights guaranteed in Article 6 are never withdrawn as a punishment.

No constitution can guarantee a world without crime. This one removes its main causes (poverty, insecurity, inequality, war) and keeps fair justice for what remains.

Article 9 — Rights cannot be reduced. No law, allocation decision, sanction against a region, or emergency may reduce the rights of Article 6 below their guaranteed level.

Article 9a — Emergencies. (Follows Article 9.)

  1. Who declares. The Assembly of the Peoples (by majority) and the Council of Regions (by a majority of regions) declare a state of emergency, on a proposal of the Coordination Council. If they cannot meet within 72 hours, the Coordination Council may declare one for at most 72 hours.
  2. How long. An emergency lasts at most 30 days. Each renewal, for at most 30 days, needs two-thirds of the Assembly and two-thirds of regions. Without renewal it ends by itself.
  3. Where. It applies only to the regions and matters affected.
  4. Court review. The Court reviews every declaration and renewal within 7 days. It may end them or any measure under them.
  5. Never. No emergency may:
  6. suspend or delay an election or a referendum;
  7. dissolve or suspend the Assembly, the Council of Regions, the Court, a regional government or any body of Article 22a;
  8. reduce the rights of Article 6 (Article 9);
  9. change this Constitution or any law by decree;
  10. freeze accounts or open personal data without a court order (Article 12a);
  11. censor information (Article 6a);
  12. use the Force against peaceful people.
  13. Publish. Every emergency measure is published within 24 hours, with its reasons and end date. All measures end with the emergency.
  14. No amendments. This Constitution may not be amended during an emergency or within 6 months after it.
  15. Brake. More than 90 days of emergency in any year triggers the brakes of Article 39a.

Basis: democracies are 75% more likely to erode under a state of emergency (v79/power evidence row 24). Only 55% of national emergency clauses set a time limit, and nearly 40% allow decrees with force of law (v79/power evidence row 25). Delaying elections is a common and usually successful evasion (v79/power evidence row 18). The 30-day limit and supermajority renewal come from the author's direction E; the exact numbers are judgement.

Part III — The economy of One Earth

Article 10 — Needs first, by population. Each year the Allocation Authority (Article 26) directs new capacity to regions by measured needs and number of people, needs first, using a public formula. New capacity includes:

  • energy systems;
  • homes;
  • health workers trained in-region;
  • food;
  • materials.

Surplus beyond a region's needs goes to the One Earth Fund for sharing.

Evidence: needs-first allocation closes the world's energy, health and housing gaps within about 30 years; a market economy with the same resources does not (V6.8). The advantage survives realistic errors and leakage when safeguards are in place (V6.9).

Article 10a — The allocation formula. (Gives the public formula of Article 10.)

  1. For each need (energy, health care, homes, food, education), each region's gap is the published target per person minus its measured level, times its population.
  2. Targets take account of real differences in need, such as age, climate, disability and remoteness. Each adjustment is published with its evidence and approved by the Scientific Council.
  3. Health need is weighted by both the age of the population and its burden of disease (healthy years of life lost), in equal parts. Age alone is never used, because it would send care away from young regions with the most disease.
  4. Decent for everyone first. New capacity first closes every region's gap to the decent level. Only then does it go to the comfortable level. Within each step, it goes to the largest gaps first, in proportion to need.
  5. Where to produce. One Earth decides where goods are produced so as to use the fewest working hours, resources and transport, while giving every region productive work.
  6. The formula, its data and each year's results are published in the Register (Article 33). Any region may challenge them before the Court.

Evidence: in the 30-year world plan, serving the decent level first brings every region to decent energy in 4 years instead of 8, decent health care in 7 instead of 12, and decent housing in 9 instead of 20, with no loss on the comfortable goal. Planning where food comes from cuts transport by 69% (V7.9, v79/allocation). Weighting health need by age alone would give Sub-Saharan Africa 30% of new health workers and 116 per 10,000 by year 30, against 151 in Europe; weighting by age and disease burden together gives 35%, and 142 against 141 (v79/health_need, WHO healthy life expectancy).

Article 10b — Producing what people want. (Ordinary goods, beyond the needs of Article 10a.)

  1. Production of ordinary goods follows what people ask for: what they buy, plus what they order when a product is out of stock. Orders are kept for up to three months and are published, without personal data.
  2. Forecasts and production plans are updated every month, never only once a year. A plan is never built by adding a target to last year's plan.
  3. Each product keeps a stock sized from its measured forecast error, so that most people find what they want at once and the rest wait a short, published time.
  4. An order is a commitment: it is paid when placed and refunded in full if the product is not delivered. No one may place orders only to hoard or resell (Article 18b).
  5. New products start with a pilot run and grow as orders come in (Article 3d).
  6. Waiting times, lost orders and unsold stock are published for every product group each month. If more than a tenth of orders in a group lapse for six months running, the region must explain why and act.

Evidence: in a 20-year simulation of 120 products with changing tastes and the same labour hours (V8.2, v82/production), these signals served 96% of wishes within 3 months, lost 3.8% and wasted 2.8% of production. A market with flexible prices priced out 19% of wishes, almost all among the poorer half, and lost 10% more; a Soviet-style annual plan lost 47%. Without waiting lists the lost share doubled.

Article 11 — Responsible production and stewardship.

  1. Before producing new, society maintains, repairs, renovates, reuses and converts what exists.
  2. Nothing is produced only to sustain sales. There is no waste and no overproduction.
  3. Whole-life environmental impacts are assessed.
  4. Each region keeps studied, rotated reserves against catastrophes: at least three months of essential goods (food, medicines, energy, materials for repair). Evidence: a spending rush of a quarter of all free money would add about 3.2 months of goods to demand (V7.9, V8.2).

Article 12 — One currency, the U.

  1. One Earth has one digital currency, the U. There is no cash of any kind: no banknotes, no coins. Every payment is digital and made online.
  2. Every commercial transaction carries a verifiable invoice through the global transaction network. Personal gifts need none.
  3. The system guarantees:
    • privacy;
    • access without a smartphone;
    • access everywhere, through several independent networks, so that no single failure stops payments;
    • independent oversight;
    • the right to appeal.

Article 12a — Your money and your data are yours. (Follows Article 12. Gives detail to 12.3 and to Article 37's "as the law allows". Amends 12.2 as in point 5.)

  1. No freeze without a judge. An account may be frozen, seized or restricted only by the order of a court, for a named person, a stated offence and a limited time. The person is told and may appeal. No administrative body, emergency or model may do it.
  2. Needs are never blocked. Even under a court order, every person can always pay for food, home, health, energy and transport at the level of Article 6.
  3. No data without a judge. Personal payment data are opened only by a court order about a named person. Every access is logged. The person is told after the investigation ends, and at the latest after one year. The number of accesses is published each year.
  4. Private everyday payments.
  5. Everyday payments by a person are anonymous to everyone except the payer, up to a limit per payment and per month.
  6. The starting limits are 200 U per payment and 2,000 U per month. The Monetary Authority adjusts them by published rule.
  7. The limits are never lowered in the 12 months before an election.
  8. Invoices name the seller, not the buyer. For anonymous payments, the invoice of Article 12.2 names the seller and the goods, not the buyer.
  9. Always online, never stranded. All payments are online; there are no offline payment devices. If the network fails in a region, food, health care, energy, housing and transport under Article 6 continue to be provided to everyone, and are recorded when the network returns.
  10. Where data live. Personal payment data stay in the region where the person lives. They are held on open-source, distributed systems run by at least three independent operators and audited every year. Bodies of One Earth receive only aggregated data, about groups of at least 1,000 people.
  11. Deletion. Personal transaction details are deleted after 2 years, unless a court order covers them. Enterprise accounts are kept as the law requires.
  12. No scoring. Payment data are never used to score, rank or punish people (Article 3c).

Basis: the ECB's digital euro design shows that a central digital currency can keep everyday payments private from the issuer (v79/power evidence row 37), and BIS Project Tourbillon shows that payer anonymity is technically feasible in a fully digital system (row 39). In Canada in 2022, accounts were frozen without a judge under emergency powers, and a court later found this violated the right against unreasonable search and seizure (row 27).

Article 12b — Trade with countries outside One Earth.

  1. Only One Earth trades with countries that have not joined, through a public Trade Office. Persons and enterprises buy imported goods in U, at the same fixed price as the same goods made inside One Earth.
  2. One Earth sells exports to non-members at fair world prices and keeps the foreign currency in one common account. Over five years, imports are paid for by exports, without new debt (Transitional provision T-4).
  3. Imports for needs come first: energy, medicines, food and the equipment for needs-first capacity (Article 10a).
  4. One Earth keeps a reserve of foreign currency covering at least six months of essential imports.
  5. The U is not traded on currency markets. Personal conversion into foreign currency is allowed for travel and family abroad, up to published limits.
  6. Trade with non-members is fair: no dumping and no hidden subsidies, and every contract is published in the Register (Article 33).

Evidence (V8.2, v82/trade): France imports 94% of its mining products and energy raw materials, 89% of its electronics and 88% of its chemicals. Essential imports were €239 billion in 2022, 36% of all imports; its exports (€925 billion) cover them 3.9 times, and a six-month reserve is about €119 billion.

Article 13 — Fixed prices.

  1. Prices of essentials are fixed permanently.
  2. They are set from the human work and resources each good embodies.
  3. Gains in productivity become shorter working hours, never higher prices or unemployment.
  4. New products. The price of a new product is the hours of work it embodies, including all inputs, times the fixed price of one hour. It is set once, when the product is introduced, and published with its calculation.
  5. The price of one hour is fixed forever.
  6. Energy for needs is cheap, and its price rises in steps. Every person has an essential energy allowance for home electricity, heating, cooking and essential travel, set each year from measured needs by the Scientific Council.
    • The allowance starts at four-fifths of the region's average home energy use per person when it joins (in France about 5,000 kWh a year, about 414 kWh a month) and is lowered as homes are renovated, toward about 3,000 kWh. Essential travel is covered by free public transport for needs and a mobility allowance.
    • Within the allowance, electricity, gas and fuels cost only the work they embody, with no surcharge (about 72 U a month for an all-electric home at the full allowance).
    • Up to one and a half times the allowance, half the resource surcharge applies.
    • Beyond that, the full resource surcharge applies.
    • Energy used to grow, process and transport food is never surcharged, so that food stays cheap everywhere. The same holds for energy used by health care, schools and public transport.
  7. Nature has a price too. Energy used beyond the allowance, air travel, and goods that use scarce natural resources (rare materials, mining products, land) carry a resource surcharge, measured physically, so that they never become cheaper, relative to other goods, than they are when a region joins. The surcharge goes to the One Earth Fund.
  8. Rare goods. What cannot be produced again, such as an original work of art or a seat at a unique event, is allocated by turn, lottery or rotation, never by bidding.

Evidence: priced by hours alone, French fuels would become 79% cheaper and electricity 65% cheaper relative to other goods, because their market prices pay mostly for resources, not work. That would encourage waste, so the surcharge is essential (V7.9, v79/prices). The calculator of V8.1 (v81/prices) sets the surcharges at +507% for fuels, +271% for electricity and gas, +98% for air travel and +75% for mining products, which keeps each at today's relative price. Within the essential allowance, a kilowatt-hour of electricity costs about 0.17 U and a litre of fuel about 0.8 U; if the allowance covers 80% of today's household energy, the household basket is only about 1.5% dearer, against 6% with the surcharge on all energy. Cheap energy beyond needs is used more (V7.1), which is why the surcharge remains above the allowance. Examples at the base salary: a kilo of tomatoes about 10 U (16 minutes of work), a litre of petrol about 4.7 U, a small new car about 23,000 U (4.6 months).

Article 14 — Salaries.

  1. The base salary is 5,000 U a month.
  2. Salaries rise with experience, knowledge and the complexity of the work, up to 5 times the base (25,000 U).
  3. The pay of enterprise owners and managers is a salary within the same limit.
  4. Average pay may rise only as fast as productivity.
  5. No exception. No bonus, prize, profit share or reward of any kind lifts a person's monthly income above five times the base salary (25,000 U).

Article 14a — Hard and unwanted work.

  1. Hardship steps. A published score for night, weekend, physical, dangerous or unpleasant work adds up to half a multiplier step to a person's salary, always within five times the base salary (Article 14.5).
  2. Shorter hours. The hardest jobs count up to 5 fewer hours a week at the same pay.
  3. Duty to improve. Every hard job has a yearly plan to make it less hard (staffing, equipment, organisation, machines for heavy tasks), published and audited.
  4. Training. Anyone moving into a job where the region lacks workers is trained on full pay.
  5. Review. If a sector stays short of workers for two years, its hardship step, hours and conditions are reviewed automatically.

Evidence: in a job-choice model of 20,000 people using France's sector sizes (V8.2, v82/jobs), paying only for knowledge left 87% of people in hard jobs there unwillingly. Hardship steps, shorter hours and work made 30% less hard together brought this to 46%, about the level of ordinary jobs, at the cost of a higher average multiplier (1.60 to 1.85) and about 9% more people in hard sectors. In France the hardest jobs to fill in 2025 were hotels and restaurants (4.5% vacancies), personal services, health and social care, and construction (Eurostat).

Article 14b — Retirement.

  1. Every retired person receives a pension of at least 2,500 U a month, plus 50 U a month for each year worked beyond twenty, up to 3,750 U.
  2. The retirement account of Article 17.4 is paid out on top, evenly from retirement.
  3. Housing, health care and care are guaranteed and free. Disability and high care needs receive case-by-case top-ups.
  4. The amounts are fixed by law and reviewed every five years against the cost of the needs basket.

Article 15 — Children.

  1. Each child brings an allowance of 1,000 U a month, limited so that adult household income plus child allowance does not exceed 15,000 U.
  2. The disability guarantee counts as adult income for this limit but is never reduced by it.

Article 16 — Enterprises.

  1. Enterprises are free to organise and innovate. There is no commercial competition for profit; ideas and methods may be compared openly.
  2. Enterprises first pay for production, maintenance and investment.
  3. Their surplus goes to the region, and the region's surplus to the One Earth Fund.
  4. Open accounts in U. Every enterprise publishes yearly accounts in U: sales, inputs, rent of premises, energy, wear of equipment, salaries and the community share it returns.
  5. Quiet periods. When demand falls, the region covers an enterprise's losses for up to six months from the pooled community shares. After that, staff and capacity move to where demand is (Article 10b), with no loss of income for the workers. Persistent losses lead to a public review of the enterprise, never to the ruin of the people in it.

Evidence (V8.2, v82/enterprise): with France's real cost structures, a 10-person restaurant serving about 62,000 meals a year at 51 U each pays its food, rent (about 4,350 U a month), energy, equipment and UNIRA salaries (average 1.9×, owner-chef 3×) and returns a community share of 24% of sales; a 40-person supermarket returns 36%. They break even at 63% and 54% of normal demand.

Article 16a — Investment, machines and infrastructure.

  1. Maintenance first. Each year, every region invests at least what wears out, measured in hours of work, so that homes, networks, machines and public buildings are never run down.
  2. Then needs. New capacity goes first to unmet needs, under the formula of Article 10a.
  3. Then saving hours. Machines and methods that save more hours over their life than they take to build are chosen by their published hours payback, within ecological limits. The hours they save shorten the working week (Article 13.3); they never raise output beyond needs.
  4. Who decides. The Allocation Authority proposes the yearly investment plan; regions decide a published share for local priorities. Every project, its cost in hours and its expected savings are published in the Register (Article 33).
  5. Checked afterwards. Each project's real savings are compared with its promise. Teams whose projects save less than promised lose priority for new ones; teams that save more are recognised (Article 3d).

Evidence (V8.2, v82/capital): in France, investment is 22.2% of GDP and depreciation 18.4%, so 83% of investment only replaces wear; investment goods take about 17% of all working hours. Directing today's net investment (about 3% of hours) to machines that repay their hours in 6–10 years shortens the average week from 30 to about 27–28 hours within 20 years.

Article 17 — Savings and the money cap.

  1. Savings are free and protected, up to a personal money cap set each year by the Monetary Authority under Article 17b, so that people keep working to produce what is needed.
  2. The cap applies only to savings held, never to salary or spending. Every person always receives their full salary and may spend it.
  3. A moderate cap. The cap is never below one and a half years of base salary (90,000 U).
  4. Protected accounts outside the cap. Every person may also hold, outside the cap:
    • a home account, until they own a home, of up to 150,000 U, used to buy or build it;
    • a retirement account of up to 120,000 U, paid out evenly from retirement as income on top of the needs-based pension;
    • a time account, for salaried people, of up to 13 weeks of their own pay, used for paid leave of at most 2 weeks a year or saved for a sabbatical of up to 3 months. In jobs where the region lacks workers, the time is banked and taken later.
  5. Order. Savings first fill the free account up to the cap. Money above the cap then fills the home, retirement and time accounts, in that order. Only what remains, including large inheritances, goes to the One Earth Fund. The limits are fixed by law and published. If total hours worked fall below the region's needs, the time-account limit is lowered automatically.
  6. Homes are not money and are never taken.

Evidence (V7.9, v79/money/cap_level.py, with home, retirement and time accounts):

Higher caps protect a few more savers but sharply raise the risk of empty shelves at fixed prices; protected accounts cannot be spent in a rush. A person can keep up to 360,000 U plus their time account. No salaried person was blocked from a first home in any case tested. The time account (2 weeks a year) costs 1.8% of working time and lowers the One Earth Fund's income from 22.0% to 19.8% of consumption (v79/money/time_account.py). Without any cap, unspent money grows without limit at fixed prices (V6.4).

Article 17a — The One Earth Fund.

  1. The One Earth Fund receives:
    • money above the personal cap;
    • large inheritances above the cap;
    • regional surpluses beyond needs.
  2. It pays for what One Earth allocates to every region:
    • new energy systems, homes and infrastructure;
    • the training of health workers and teachers;
    • the guarantees of Article 6 wherever a region's own means fall short;
    • the catastrophe reserves;
    • debts owed to outside creditors during the transition.
  3. Everything the Fund receives and spends is published in the Public Allocation Register (Article 33).

This is why money above the cap goes to One Earth: it returns, as resources, to the regions that need them.

Article 17b — The Monetary Authority: rules, not discretion. (Follows Article 17a. Amends Article 17.1: "set each year by a published coefficient" becomes "set each year by the Monetary Authority under Article 17b".)

  1. The Monetary Authority has five members from five continents. The Court appoints them from a public shortlist drawn up by the Scientific Council. They serve one term of 7 years.
  2. Its only tasks are:
  3. to keep the U working;
  4. to apply the fixed hour price of Article 13;
  5. to compute the money cap of Article 17 each year by the published thermostat rule;
  6. to set the anonymous-payment limits of Article 12a by rule.
  7. It has no discretion. It may not:
  8. set prices;
  9. allocate resources;
  10. lend;
  11. create U except by published rule;
  12. treat any person, enterprise or region differently from others.
  13. The thermostat rule. Each year the cap is multiplied by the square root of (target stock of unspent money ÷ actual stock), within at most 10% up or down, and never set below the moderate cap of Article 17.3. The target is set by law (12 months of the region's consumption in the research model). Anyone can recompute the result from public data.
  14. One cap for everyone. The same cap applies to every person in every region.
  15. Changing the rule. The rule changes only by the procedure of Article 26a.3. A change never takes effect in the 12 months before an Assembly election.
  16. The Audit Authority checks each year's figure. If a figure differs from what the rule gives, the rule's figure applies, and the Court enforces it.
  17. The Authority holds no personal payment data (Article 12a).

Basis: political control of money is associated with worse outcomes (v79/power evidence row 35), and committed rules beat discretion (v79/power evidence row 36). These findings come from inflation-targeting central banks. The U has fixed prices, so the risk here is political use of the cap, not inflation. The evidence transfers by analogy only.

Evidence: in a 60-year simulation with a disaster, a wave of precautionary saving, a baby boom and fast automation, this rule kept unspent money within 10–14 months of consumption and no salaried person was ever blocked from a first home. Without the 10% limit the cap could fall 88% in a single year (V7.9, v79/money).

Article 18 — Homes.

  1. Homes are priced between 100,000 and 500,000 U according to size and location.
  2. An adult may own at most two homes, and a family at most two in total.
  3. Zero-interest credit is available, repaid from salary.
  4. A home inherited beyond the heir's first returns to the regional pool or is chosen by the heir.
  5. Public housing is guaranteed for anyone without a salary.
  6. New homes are built with low-carbon materials where possible.

7. Allocation where demand exceeds supply. When more households apply for homes in a zone than there are homes free, the homes are allocated by published priority points. Points are given for:

  • household size and children;
  • nearness of the home to the household's place of work or study;
  • disability and care duties;
  • time already waiting;
  • local roots.

Income, wealth and any offer to pay more are never taken into account.

8. Public lottery. Applicants whose points fall in the same published band are ranked by a public lottery, drawn once a year for all zones together. One-tenth of these homes go to an open lottery among all applicants, so that nobody is shut out; a region may raise this to at most one-fifth. No one may buy, sell or transfer a place in the ranking.

9. Must live in. In a zone declared high-demand, a home must be the main residence of the household that holds it. It may not be left empty, used as a second home, or let out whole. A home left empty for more than six months without a recognised reason (care, study, work posting, repair) returns to the regional pool. The second home allowed by Article 18.2 may not be in a high-demand zone.

10. Checks. Points claims are checked before a home is handed over. A household that made a false claim loses the offer and may not apply for five years.

11. Building to need. Each region publishes waiting times and points thresholds for every zone each year. Where the average wait in a zone exceeds five years, the region builds more homes there and moves jobs and services towards other zones until waits fall.

Evidence: in a 30-year simulation of a city with 2.5 applicants per desirable home, points with a lottery housed 28% of high-need households within 5 years (0% for a queue), with no income gradient; bidding left the poorer half with almost nothing; the must-live-in rule cut empty desirable homes from 6–32% to under 0.5% (V7.9, v79/housing).

Article 18b — Second-hand goods and resale.

  1. Using goods again is encouraged (Article 11). Anyone may sell a used good through the public second-hand platform of the region.
  2. A used good is sold at most at its fixed price minus a published wear schedule for its kind (for example a fixed share per year of use). Higher prices, bidding and private auctions are not allowed.
  3. Goods obtained through a waiting list or an allocation may not be resold within one year, except back to the public pool at their worn value.
  4. No one may buy goods in order to resell them. Repeated resale beyond personal use is reviewed, and the margin goes to the One Earth Fund.
  5. Homes follow Article 18. Rare goods follow Article 13.8.

Basis: fixed prices with waiting lists invite resale at a premium, as in shortage economies; capped resale prices and minimum holding periods are used for allocated public housing (for example Singapore's minimum occupation periods, v79/housing/evidence.csv).

Article 19 — Metering. Energy, travel and data are always metered and paid per unit, never at a flat rate. Within the essential energy allowance (Article 13.6), each unit is paid at the hours price only.

Evidence: free or flat-rate access raises use of goods without natural limits by 14–70%; a 20–30% energy rebound would break the climate limit (V7.1).

Article 20 — No public debt. No region carries public debt.

  1. Debts between members are cancelled.
  2. Bonds held by savers and pension funds count as their protected savings.
  3. Debts to outside creditors are paid on schedule until they join.

Article 21 — Within Earth's limits.

  1. Total energy use, emissions, materials, land and water stay within limits set from the best science.
  2. Regions using more than a comfortable level of energy reduce their use each year while regions below it rise.

Evidence: 40 GJ of final energy per person for everyone fits a 1.5 °C pathway with regions above it reducing about 3–4% a year (V6.6, V6.7).

Part IV — Institutions: one strong Earth, no ruling region

Article 22 — Separation of powers. No person or body holds more than one of these powers:

  • making law;
  • carrying it out;
  • allocating resources;
  • measuring needs;
  • auditing;
  • judging.

Article 22a — No body holds arms, money and allocation together. (Follows Article 22. Adds "armed force", "money" and "science" to the powers that Article 22 separates.)

  1. These powers are held by separate bodies:

Article 23 — The Assembly of the Peoples.

  1. The Assembly has 1,000 members, elected every 5 years by direct, secret, universal vote.
  2. Seats are shared among regions in proportion to the square root of their population (the Penrose rule, which gives every citizen equal voting power in a union of regions). Every region has at least 1 seat, and no region more than 5% of all seats.

Test on 2024 populations (v75/constitution/voting.py): the largest regions, India and China, each get about 4.4% of seats, against 16–17% under pure proportionality.

Article 24 — The Council of Regions.

  1. Each region sends one representative with one equal vote, whatever its size or wealth.

Article 25 — How laws are made.

  1. A law passes with a majority in the Assembly of the Peoples and a double majority in the Council of Regions:
    • at least 55% of regions;
    • representing at least 65% of the world's population, counted by the square root of each region's population, as in Article 23.
  2. Neither the largest regions nor the smallest can decide alone.

Test on 2024 populations: passing a law needs at least 120 of 217 regions; blocking one needs at least 21 (v75/outputs/voting.json). Counting raw population instead would let India and China together (35.2% of humanity) block any law alone. The thresholds (55%/65%) follow the Council of the European Union and were agreed by the author on 2026-11-03.

Article 26 — The Allocation Authority.

  1. It applies the public allocation formula of Article 10 to the needs measured by the Statistical Office.
  2. It has no power to favour any region.
  3. Changing the formula requires a law under Article 25.

Article 26a — Allocation without favour. (Follows Article 26. Replaces 26.3 and limits Article 38.)

  1. The Allocation Authority has nine members from different continents, elected for one 5-year term (Article 22a).
  2. Needs only. The formula of Article 10 depends only on measured needs and population. No allocation may depend on how a region or its people vote, on their politics or opinions, or on loyalty to any body.
  3. Changing the formula needs:
  4. two-thirds of the Assembly;
  5. a double majority of the Council of Regions;
  6. a published opinion of the Scientific Council;
  7. review by a citizens' assembly (Article 32b).

A change takes effect only after the next Assembly election.

  1. Formula against actual. Each year the Register (Article 33) shows, for every region, what the formula gives and what was allocated. A region whose gap exceeds 2% receives reasons within 30 days. Any region or citizen may ask the Court to correct it.
  2. Discretionary allocations are at most 2% of the yearly total and follow a published rule. Loss of priority under Article 38 needs a ruling of the Court.
  3. Honest statistics. The Statistical Office publishes its raw data and code. The Audit Authority and any region may replicate its figures. A difference of more than 5% goes to the Court.
  4. No region's allocation is cut as a sanction except by ruling of the Court. It never falls below what Article 6 requires.

Article 27 — The Coordination Council (executive).

  1. Seven to fifteen coordinators, from different regions, are elected by the Assembly for a single 5-year term.
  2. The chair rotates every year.
  3. No region may hold the chair twice in a row.

Article 28 — The Statistical Office. It measures needs in every region at least once a year with stated uncertainty, under public, uniform methods, and is independent of all other bodies (Article 37).

Article 29 — The Audit Authority. It audits every region every year (Article 36). Its budget is fixed by this Constitution as a share of allocated value.

Article 30 — Terms and rotation.

  1. No person holds a One Earth office for more than 10 years in total.
  2. All office-holders declare their assets publicly each year and are subject to the money cap.
  3. No one may decide an allocation to a region where they or their family hold an interest.

Article 30a — Limits on office. (Follows Article 30. Adds to 30.1–30.3.)

  1. Terms. No person holds a One Earth office for more than 10 years in a lifetime. Executive, allocation, monetary, command and judicial offices are held for one term only.
  2. No benefit to oneself. A change to the length of terms, the number of terms or the pay of an office never applies to anyone holding that office, or elected to it, when the change is adopted.
  3. Pay. Office-holders are paid within the Article 14 scale, from 1 to 5 times the base salary. They receive no other income and no gifts above a small value fixed by law.
  4. Assets. Office-holders declare their assets, and those of their household, each year while in office and for 5 years after. The Audit Authority checks the declarations and they are published.
  5. After office. For 5 years after leaving office, no one may:
  6. lobby the body they served in;
  7. work for a region or enterprise they decided about.

Judges and members of the Monetary Authority and the Command Board hold no other public office or paid post for 5 years after their term.

  1. No families in power. No close relative of an office-holder serves in the same body at the same time.
  2. Elections on time. Elections to the Assembly and to regional parliaments are held on fixed dates and are never delayed. If a disaster makes voting impossible in a region, that region votes within 90 days, and no other region's vote is delayed.

Article 31 — Regions.

  1. Each region has its own democratic government and parliament, elected by its people, with full powers under Article 4.
  2. Regional governments cannot be dissolved by One Earth except by a ruling of the Court for a grave breach of rights.

Article 32 — The Court of One Earth.

  1. Judges are appointed for one 12-year term by the Council of Regions from candidates proposed by an independent judicial commission, and are drawn from all continents.
  2. The Court:
    • protects rights;
    • settles disputes between regions;
    • reviews laws and allocations against this Constitution.
  3. Every person may bring a case.

Article 32a — An independent Court. (Follows Article 32. Adds to 32.1.)

  1. The Court has 21 judges, at most one from any region and at least three from each continent. This number is fixed by this Constitution.
  2. One-third of the judges are replaced every 4 years, so that no single majority appoints the whole Court.
  3. The judicial commission of Article 32.1 has three equal parts:
  4. judges of regional high courts, drawn by lot;
  5. members nominated by regional bar associations and law faculties;
  6. citizens drawn by lot.

No office-holder sits on it.

  1. A judge may be removed only for grave misconduct or incapacity, by two-thirds of the other judges and two-thirds of the Council of Regions.
  2. The Court's budget is a fixed share of One Earth's administrative budget, set in this Constitution. Judges' pay is never reduced during their term.
  3. No law may remove a matter from the Court's jurisdiction. Every body obeys its rulings.
  4. The Court rules within 7 days on emergencies, deployments, account freezes and exit disputes.

Basis: established independent courts prevent slides to authoritarianism, but new courts are fragile (v79/power evidence rows 22, 23). Courts were used in 30% of successful term-limit overstays (v79/power evidence row 19). So the Court's independence must be built into its structure, not trusted.

Article 32b — Citizens' assemblies by lot. (Follows Article 32a.)

  1. Citizens' Council.
  2. A permanent Citizens' Council of 100 people is drawn by lot each year. It is balanced by region, sex, age and education.
  3. It may place items on the agenda of the Assembly, and the Assembly must debate them within 6 months.
  4. Review of major decisions. A citizens' assembly of at least 500 people, drawn by lot in the same way, reviews:
  5. every change to the allocation formula, the thermostat rule or this Constitution;
  6. every emergency renewal beyond 60 days;
  7. every Force deployment beyond 90 days;
  8. every trigger of Article 39a.
  9. If two-thirds of the citizens' assembly oppose a decision, the decision is suspended. It takes effect only if the Assembly then confirms it by two-thirds.
  10. Members receive full pay while they serve. They are protected against any pressure, and they serve once in a lifetime.

Basis: lot-based assemblies are now common (289 cases catalogued by the OECD, E 31). They have been made permanent in places such as Ostbelgien (v79/power evidence row 32). In Ireland they opened a decision that politicians had avoided (v79/power evidence rows 33, 34). There is no evidence yet that they stop capture at world scale. Their role here as a brake is judgement.

Part V — Safeguards

Article 33 — Public Allocation Register. Every allocation is published within 30 days in a free, open register stating what, where, how much, why and on which data. Personal data are never published. Evidence: when grant amounts were published in Uganda, the share captured before reaching schools fell from about 80% to under 20% (Reinikka & Svensson 2004).

Article 34 — Direct digital delivery and verification.

  1. Payments go directly, in U, to those who deliver.
  2. Delivery is verified on site and by remote sensing before it is recorded as complete.

Evidence: digital payment cut leakage by 19–24% in India.

Article 35 — Community monitoring and whistleblowers.

  1. Every community may monitor allocations and delivery.
  2. People who report misuse are protected.
  3. Every person may complain and receive a reasoned answer within 90 days.

Article 36 — Independent audits. Every region is audited every year, at random and without notice. All reports are public. Evidence: audits reduced missing funds by 8 percentage points (Indonesia; Olken 2007) and corruption by about 8%, 13% after repeated audits (Brazil; Avis, Ferraz & Finan 2018).

Article 37 — Timely and honest data.

  1. Needs data are less than one year old.
  2. Data are checked against independent sources.
  3. Regions may not change methods alone.

Article 38 — Correction without punishing people. Misallocated resources are recovered and reallocated. A region found misreporting loses priority for discretionary allocations, never its people's rights.

Article 39 — Built-in test of the system.

  1. World leakage and data age are published each year.
  2. If leakage exceeds 25% or data are more than 2 years old, the Assembly must review the allocation system within a year.
  3. If leakage exceeds 45% or data are more than 5 years old for 3 consecutive years, the allocation system must be reformed by law.

Evidence: above those levels, needs-first allocation loses its measured advantage (V6.9).

Article 39a — Democracy health check and automatic brakes. (Follows Article 39.)

  1. The Democracy Monitor.
  2. Each year an independent Democracy Monitor publishes indicators, with their uncertainty, for One Earth and for each region.
  3. The Monitor is a panel of research institutes from at least five continents, chosen by lot from qualified applicants.
  4. It is paid from a fixed share and does not depend on any office-holder.
  5. The indicators cover at least:
  6. elections that are free, fair and on time;
  7. freedom of the media and of expression;
  8. independence of the Court and whether its rulings are obeyed;
  9. freedom of association and civil society;
  10. account freezes and data accesses (counts);
  11. emergency days;
  12. Force deployments and complaints;
  13. leakage and the formula gap (Article 26a.4);
  14. how concentrated offices are among persons and groups.
  15. Methods are public. A change of method needs the Scientific Council's approval, and past years are recalculated with it.
  16. Brakes. If the overall index, or the indicator for elections, media or the Court, falls by more than its uncertainty for two years running, these follow automatically:
  17. extra audits of every body within 6 months;
  18. a citizens' assembly (Article 32b), which reports on causes and remedies; the Assembly votes on its proposals within 6 months;
  19. all emergency powers end, and no new ones may be declared for 12 months, except for disaster relief that restricts no rights;
  20. early Assembly elections within 6 months, unless the citizens' assembly finds by two-thirds that the fall came from causes outside the control of One Earth's bodies.
  21. A fall in one region triggers extra audits and a citizens' assembly in that region. It never allows One Earth to dissolve the regional government (Article 31.2).
  22. No body may suspend or delay these brakes.

Basis: V-Dem shows that democratic decline can be measured each year, with uncertainty, by indicator (v79/power evidence row 1–6). That freedom of expression falls first (v79/power evidence row 6) is why media is a trigger indicator. No existing constitution has automatic brakes like these, so the mechanism is untested judgement.

Part VI — Joining, leaving and changing this Constitution

Article 40 — Joining.

  1. A region joins by referendum of its people.
  2. It passes through three stages:
    • observer, building the safeguard institutions;
    • associate, receiving needs-based transfers and adopting the 30-hour week;
    • full member, adopting the U and all rules.

Article 41 — Leaving. A region may leave by referendum, with a negotiated settlement. Every person may leave any region at any time; entry elsewhere follows Article 4b.

Article 41a — Leaving One Earth. (Follows Article 41. Gives the procedure for its first sentence.)

  1. Request. A region asks to leave by:
  2. a decision of its parliament by majority; or
  3. a citizens' initiative signed by 10% of its voters.
  4. Referendum. The region holds a referendum within 2 years of the request. It is supervised by the Court and by observers from other regions.
  5. Threshold. The region leaves if a majority of votes cast says so, with turnout of at least 50% of voters. No higher threshold may be set after the request.
  6. Default date. Exit takes effect 2 years after the vote, or earlier or later if both sides agree. One Earth cannot block it by refusing to agree.
  7. Fair share.
  8. Assets built in the region stay with it.
  9. The region receives its population share of the common reserves and of the One Earth Fund's liquid assets, minus commitments still due.
  10. If there is no agreement within 18 months, the Court decides by a published formula.
  11. No force, no punishment. Until exit, allocations and Article 6 rights continue in full. The Peace Force leaves the region before exit, with its heavy weapons. Force, threats of force, or cutting allocations against a region that asks or votes to leave are forbidden.
  12. People first. Every person in a leaving region may keep residence or move to a member region. People belonging to minorities keep the protection of Article 4a until the new arrangements protect them as well.
  13. No endless votes. If a vote to leave fails, no new request may be made for 5 years, unless two-thirds of the regional parliament ask for one.

Basis: Article 50 TEU shows that a default exit date stops the centre from stalling (v79/power evidence row 43). Nevis shows that a supermajority set by the centre can override a clear majority (v79/power evidence row 44). Ethiopia's Article 39 provides a full procedure with a deadline and asset division (v79/power evidence row 42). Whether exit rights reduce or increase conflict is NOT established by the evidence gathered here. In Ethiopia a written exit right did not prevent war (not verified here). Exit is protected here because, with only one armed force, it is the last check on capture.

Article 42 — Amendments.

  1. This Constitution is amended by a two-thirds majority in the Assembly of the Peoples, a double majority of 65% of regions representing 65% of the population in the Council of Regions, and a world referendum.
  2. Article 6 rights and Article 9 may never be reduced by amendment.

Article 42a — The protected core. (Follows Article 42. Extends 42.2.)

  1. No amendment may abolish or weaken:
  2. the rights of Article 6, Article 9 and Article 4a.2;
  3. free, secret, universal elections on fixed dates, at least every 5 years, for the Assembly and every regional parliament;
  4. term limits (Articles 27, 30 and 30a);
  5. the separation of powers and the independence of the Court (Articles 22, 22a, 32 and 32a);
  6. the ceilings of the Peace Force, and the ban on using it against peaceful citizens, elections or a region's vote to leave (Articles 5a.1, 5a.4, 5b and 5c);
  7. the court-order protection of money and data (Article 12a.1–3);
  8. the limits on emergencies (Article 9a);
  9. the right of persons and regions to leave (Articles 4b.2, 41 and 41a);
  10. the equality of regions, with no permanent seats or vetoes (Article 2);
  11. this Article and Articles 42 and 42b.
  12. Amendments may strengthen these guarantees.
  13. Before any referendum on an amendment, the Court rules on whether it touches the core. An amendment that does is void.

Article 42b — Safe amendment. (Follows Article 42a. Adds to 42.1.)

  1. Besides the majorities of Article 42.1, the world referendum must be won by a majority of voters and in a majority of regions.
  2. An amendment to Parts IV, V or VI needs two votes of the Assembly, separated by an Assembly election.
  3. A citizens' assembly reviews every amendment (Article 32b).
  4. No amendment may be made during an emergency or within 6 months after one. No amendment may benefit anyone holding office when it is adopted (Article 30a.2).

Basis: two-thirds of term-limit evasions use amendment (v79/power evidence rows 16, 20), and courts often helped (v79/power evidence row 19). Unamendable cores are now in 53% of new constitutions (v79/power evidence row 30) and follow the German model (v79/power evidence row 28). There is little evidence on whether eternity clauses actually stop backsliding, as opposed to being common. So the core is paired with Court review, citizens' review and slow two-step votes.

Transitional provisions

T-1. Conversion.

  1. Wages move directly onto the UNIRA scale at full membership. Prices of essentials are set from embodied work. There is no single political exchange rate.
  2. Savings convert at local basket parity.
  3. The money cap phases in over 10 years. Money above it goes to the One Earth Fund. The cap applies to wealth held on the day of the joining vote.

T-2. Homes and mortgages.

  1. Homes are kept.
  2. Homes beyond two may be sold to the regional pool within 10 years.
  3. Mortgages become zero-interest U credit.

T-3. Enterprises. Owners receive their share in U under the cap. Old property claims are settled by compensation, never by giving property back.

T-4. Debts.

  1. Members' public debts are cancelled.
  2. Bonds held by savers and pension funds count as their savings.
  3. Outside creditors are paid on a schedule.

T-5. Disarmament. Armed forces are dissolved within 5 years of full membership, and every member is offered work, training and full income.

T-6. Protecting production. New members keep their productive capacity running through transitional support from the union.

T-7. Safeguards before power. (Follows T-6. Strengthens Article 5a.5.)

  1. The Court, the Audit Authority, the Inspector-General, the Democracy Monitor and the Citizens' Council are in place before the first Force unit is formed, before the U is issued and before the first allocation.
  2. In its first 5 years the Force does not exceed 250,000 members.
  3. National armies are dissolved by the same rules of mixing, so no group is stacked into the Force or purged from it. Every former soldier is offered work, training and full income (Article 5.3).

---

T-8. Equal salaries, built up within 20 years.

  1. From the first year, every person in every member region receives their needs in kind: food, health care, education, a home and the essential energy allowance.
  2. Each region's salary scale in U starts at the level its own production can support and rises every year toward the full scale of Article 14, with twenty years as the target.
  3. Salaries never rise faster than the goods available to the region, its own production plus transfers from the One Earth Fund, so that money never empties the shelves.
  4. Each region's progress is published every year. A region behind the target receives priority for investment, training and technology from One Earth.

Evidence (V8.2, v82/worldmoney): world household consumption is about $11,500 per person against about $21,900 bought by the lowest UNIRA salary, so equal salaries on day one would ask for about twice what exists. A strict 20-year schedule would leave 19–39% of demand unmet in year 20; with clause 3 there are no shortages, the One Earth Fund transfers 15–20% of world goods, and the full scale is reached in 31–39 years with strong catch-up growth.
