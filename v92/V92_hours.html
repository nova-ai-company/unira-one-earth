UNIRA V9.2 — The working week during the transition

Status (2026-11-06): preliminary, not peer reviewed; constitution unchanged pending the author's decision on the proposal in PROPOSED_CONSTITUTION_CHANGES.md. Produced with an AI assistant from the V6.2 labour model and its frozen Eurostat data. Research basis only, not independent evidence.

1. The question (author, 2026-11-06)

"In the beginning we can increase the number of hours to ensure we don't have any gap in productivity until AI replaces some stuff, and we can decrease the hours but guarantee the same salaries."

Restated as a rule that can be tested:

  1. On joining, a region's standard week starts at the shortest week its existing labour reserve can cover without any productivity gain, never above 40 hours (8 hours × 5 days).
  2. The full base salary (Article 14) is paid from the first day, whatever the length of the week.
  3. Each year the week is shortened by the region's measured productivity growth, pay unchanged (this is already Article 13.3), until it reaches the 30-hour standard of Article 6. It never rises again.

The trigger is measured productivity, from whatever source (automation, AI, organisation). Nothing is assumed about AI: if productivity does not grow, hours simply stay where they are, and that is reported, not hidden.

2. Why it matters

The paper's condition 1 (paper/UNIRA_PAPER_V1.md §3) is the one that rests on the weakest evidence: a 30-hour week from year 1 needs either a 20% rise in output per hour or an employment rate of 88%, above any country's record, and the hours–productivity elasticities are extrapolated beyond any evaluated reform (V6.2 §4c). The 35-hour comparator of V6.2 §4d was "within reach on current evidence" but was rejected as a design on 2026-11-03. The author's new direction makes the starting week a measured value and 30 hours the destination.

3. Method

hours/hours_path.py reuses the V6.2 labour-reserve model (v62/src/unira_v62/labour_reserve.py) and its manifest-verified Eurostat data for 30 European countries, employees, 2023 (2024 as a robustness year):

  • For every whole-hour cap from 30 to 40, the hours removed by the cap and the output-per-hour gain needed with no mobilisation (required_gain_no_mobilisation).
  • The existing reserve: the unemployed plus people available but not seeking (the V6.2 "slack" framing, the smaller of its two framings). Entrants work the capped week at productivity ρ relative to incumbents: 1.0 (upper bound) and 0.75 (central, conservative); both are V6.2 assumptions, not estimates.
  • Starting week = the shortest cap at which the reserve maintains the region's hours (residual gain 0).
  • Years to 30 hours when the week falls as 1/(1+g) per year with output and pay unchanged, for France's measured value-added-per-hour trends (2015–19: 0.57% a year; 2015–23: 0.06% a year, from v62/outputs/summary.json) and for illustrative rates of 1, 1.5, 2 and 3% a year (not forecasts).

Nothing is random. hours/tests/ (9 tests) checks reproducibility, agreement with the V6.2 outputs and research note, monotonicity in the cap, and the definition of the starting week.

Reading. At the productivity France actually recorded in 2015–19, a region starting at 35 h reaches 30 h in about 28 years; at the 2015–23 trend it effectively never does. The 30-hour week within a working life requires productivity growth of 1–2% a year or more, which Europe has not had since the 2000s. Whether automation and AI deliver it is unknown; the rule does not need to know, because it follows the measurement.

5. What this changes

  • The paper's condition 1 becomes weaker and better supported. "Universal work at about 88% employment" is replaced by "a starting week the region's existing reserve can cover (33–40 h in Europe, 35 h in France, measured), falling to 30 h as productivity rises". The evaluated reforms (cuts of ≤10% from ≥39 h) now cover the size of cut involved, instead of being extrapolated.
  • The falsification test on employment ("employment cannot exceed about 85%") no longer decides the design: at the starting week, the employment rate needed is today's best observed or lower.
  • The money circuit is unchanged by construction. The hour price is calibrated once, at the starting week, so that incomes buy what is produced (v64, pi = consumption / supply). Prices are set from embodied hours once (Article 13.4–13.5); when hours fall with output constant, prices and salaries stay where they are. This is reasoning on the existing rules, not a new simulation; if the proposal is adopted, V6.4 should be rerun with the starting week as a check.

6. What this does not change (honest limits)

  1. Mobilising the reserve still needs institutions. No evaluated hours cut created net jobs by itself (V6.2 §4c); take-up is a synthetic parameter. The starting week makes the task 2.5 times smaller than at 30 h, not automatic. Training, childcare, eldercare, accessible jobs and matching remain conditions.
  2. Health and care stay short at any week, because training takes years (V6.2 §4d: about 0.27 million extra people in France at 35 h, with hours–productivity elasticity near zero).
  3. The reserve is European data. The rule applies everywhere; the numbers exist only for 30 European countries. Regions with low employment and large informal sectors would start short of 40 h but their reserve and hours are not measured here.
  4. ρ is assumed. Entrants at 0.75 of incumbent productivity is illustrative; the starting week moves by about one hour between ρ = 1.0 and 0.75.
  5. Self-employed are not capped in these runs (the V6.2 primary scenario); capping them too raises the gap by about half.
  6. The arrival date at 30 h is not a promise. It depends entirely on productivity growth, which the project does not forecast. Automation effects are not assumed anywhere in this note.
  7. Fewer hours for the same pay is an increase in hourly pay. Article 14.4 ("average pay may rise only as fast as productivity") is respected: total pay is constant while productivity rises; the gain is taken as time, as Article 13.3 requires.

7. Recommendation to the author

Adopt the rule as a transitional provision (T-10) and amend the "time" right of Article 6 so that 30 hours is the standard reached through T-10, with a 40-hour ceiling and no one working more than before joining. Keep the base salary at 5,000 U from day one at any week. Set no starting hour in the constitution: it is a measured value set by law for each region (as T-8.9 does for other parameters). Do not write any AI expectation into the text.

V9.2 — proposed constitution changes: the working week during the transition

Status (2026-11-06): proposed on the author's design direction, awaiting the author's approval of the text. The direction ("start with more hours so there is no gap in productivity, shorten the week as productivity rises, guarantee the same salaries") was given by the author on 2026-11-06. The research in RESEARCH_NOTE.md gives the measured values. The text below is drafted to fit the existing articles; accept, change or reject each point separately.

What the evidence supports

  • A 30-hour week from day one is the paper's weakest condition. It needs a 20% rise in output per hour or an employment rate above any country's record, and the hours–productivity evidence is extrapolated beyond any evaluated reform (V6.2 §4c; paper §3).
  • A measured starting week removes that condition. In France the existing reserve (unemployed plus available but not seeking) maintains output at 35 h with no productivity gain; across 30 European countries the starting week is 33–40 h, never more than 40 h (V9.2 §4). Cuts of that size are within the reforms that were actually evaluated.
  • The constitution already says that productivity gains become shorter hours (Article 13.3) and that salaries are never cut (T-8.4). The proposal gives those rules a starting point and a path; it adds no new principle.
  • Strength: official hours and reserve data; the take-up of the reserve and entrant productivity are assumptions; the arrival date at 30 h depends on productivity growth that no one forecasts.

Proposal 1 — Article 6, the "time" right (amendment)

Current text.

  • time: a standard working week of 30 hours (6 hours × 5 days), so that families can live together;

Proposed text.

  • time: a standard working week of 30 hours (6 hours × 5 days), so that families can live together, reached in each region as set out in T-10; from the first day no one is required to work more than 40 hours a week, or more than before their region joined;

Why. The right stays what it is: 30 hours is the standard. The amendment says how it is reached and guarantees two things immediately: a ceiling of 8 hours × 5 days, and no increase in hours for anyone on joining (the starting week is a cap, never a requirement to work more).

Proposal 2 — New transitional provision T-10 (after T-9)

T-10. The working week during the transition. (Follows T-9. Gives effect to Articles 6 ("time"), 7, 13.3 and 14.)

  1. The starting week. When a region joins, its standard week is set by law at the shortest whole number of hours that the region's existing labour reserve can cover without loss of output, as measured by the independent body of T-8.6. It is never more than 40 hours and never more than the usual full-time week before joining. (Research values: 33–40 hours in Europe, 35 hours in France.)
  2. Full pay from the first day. The base salary of Article 14 is paid in full whatever the length of the week. Salaries are never cut when hours fall (T-8.4).
  3. Shorter each year. Each year the week is shortened by the region's measured growth in output per hour, by published rule, until it reaches 30 hours. During the transition productivity gains go to time first (Article 13.3); only once the region is at 30 hours do they go to the guaranteed income of Article 14c, if adopted.
  4. Never longer again. The week never rises, except for the duration of a declared emergency (Article 9a), under review by the Court.
  5. Jobs do not appear by themselves. The region and One Earth bring the reserve into work through training, childcare and eldercare, accessible workplaces and regional matching (Article 7, T-8.5). Where the reserve is not yet at work, the week is not shortened on paper ahead of the measurement.
  6. Published. The starting week, each reduction and the data behind them are published with the yearly report of T-8.6.

Why the safeguards. Point 1 ties the start to a measurement, not to a negotiation, and caps it so that nobody works more than today. Point 3 makes the path automatic and public, so that it cannot be postponed by discretion. Point 5 records the one finding every evaluated reform agrees on: shorter hours alone create no jobs. Point 4 protects the gain once made.

Proposal 3 — Article 40.2 (consequential)

Current text.

  • associate, receiving needs-based transfers and adopting the 30-hour week;

Proposed text.

  • associate, receiving needs-based transfers and adopting the working week of Article 6 and T-10;

Not proposed

  • No starting hour in the constitution. It is a measured value, set by law for each region, like the other parameters of T-8.9.
  • No expectation about automation or AI in the text. The rule follows measured productivity, from whatever source. If productivity does not grow, hours stay where they are, and the report of T-8.6 says so.
  • No change to the base salary (Article 14.1). The research finds that the number 5,000 U is a unit choice; what matters is what it buys, and that is fixed by the price rule.
  • No change to Article 14.4 ("average pay may rise only as fast as productivity"). Under T-10 total pay is constant while productivity rises; the gain is taken as time.

Consistency notes

  • Article 6 "time" keeps 30 hours as the right; T-10 is the path. Article 9 (rights cannot be reduced) is respected: the 40-hour ceiling and the no-increase guarantee apply from day one, and the week only ever falls.
  • Article 7 (right and duty to work) is the basis for point 5 of T-10.
  • Article 13.3–13.5 already turn productivity gains into shorter hours with prices fixed; T-10 adds the starting point and the yearly rule.
  • Article 14.1–14.5 unchanged. Full base salary at any week (T-10.2); the 5× ceiling untouched.
  • Proposed Article 14c.4 ("society shall progressively reduce compulsory working hours") is the same principle after 30 hours; T-10.3 says which comes first.
  • T-8.4 ("salaries are never cut") is restated, not changed. T-8.6 supplies the measurement and the publication. T-8.9 (parameters set by law) covers the starting week.
  • Article 42a (protected core): T-10 is transitional and is not proposed for the core.
