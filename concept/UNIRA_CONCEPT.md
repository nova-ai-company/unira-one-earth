# UNIRA — The Concept

*Consolidated normative design, 4 November 2026. This document states what UNIRA proposes. It is a design, chosen by the author; it is not a research result. What the research has tested, and how well, is stated in section 10. What the research cannot test is stated in section 11. The legal text is the Draft Constitution of One Earth (`v75/constitution/`); the evidence is in the modelling paper (`paper/`) and the research notes.*

## 1. Purpose and premises

**Purpose.** Every human being lives a comfortable, secure and fulfilling life, wherever they live, within the limits of the Earth, in a world without war. UNIRA is one way to organise an economy and a polity to that end.

**Premises.** UNIRA rests on choices that are not derived from evidence and that a reader may reject:

1. **One humanity, one federation.** The Earth is governed as one federation of equal regions (today's countries, or parts or unions of them). Regions keep their culture, religion, language, borders and daily life; they give up war, armies and the right to hold resources that others need more.
2. **Needs before wants, by population.** New capacity goes to regions by measured needs and number of people, through a public formula, not through purchasing power or negotiation.
3. **No commercial competition for profit.** Enterprises produce what is needed and wanted; ideas still compete, but not for private gain beyond a capped salary.
4. **Work shared, time protected.** Every adult able to work contributes, and the standard week is 30 hours, reached in each region from a measured starting week (T-10), so that families have time together.
5. **Money that cannot accumulate.** One digital currency, fixed prices for essentials, pay within a 1×–5× range, and a cap on personal money. Surplus returns to the community.
6. **Consent, never force.** Regions join by referendum and may leave. No one is coerced, surveilled, scored or deprived of essentials, whatever the political situation.

The premises are stated so that they can be argued with. The research does not show they are right; it tests whether the world they describe is physically possible, and what it would take.

## 2. Principles (Part I of the Constitution)

- **Fairness first** (Article 3): every public decision is guided by fairness, equality of regions and people, subsidiarity, and the limits of the Earth.
- **Data and science in every decision** (3a): decisions are based on published data and methods, with their uncertainty, reviewed by an independent Scientific Council.
- **AI serves; people decide** (3b–3c): models advise, are open and signed, must beat simple benchmarks, never score persons, and have no role in rights, sanctions or force.
- **Innovation** (3d): open public research, prizes, and rewards for visible contributions, never above the 5× ceiling.
- **Subsidiarity and diversity** (4, 4a, 4c): decisions as close to people as possible; each region's religion, culture, language, education and daily life are its own; minorities protected; no community permanently excluded from power or office within its region, each region choosing how to include its communities.
- **Borders and movement** (4b): regions control entry and settlement; everyone may leave any region; people in danger are protected first at home, then by shared shelter.
- **Peace** (5–5c): war abolished; One Earth holds the only armed force, bound by ceilings, mixed composition, many keys and lawful-orders rules; prosperity does not guarantee peace (5.6).
- **Rights that cannot be reduced** (6, 9, 9a): food and water, a home, health and education, an income, time, energy and connectivity, privacy, expression, movement and participation; emergencies are limited in time, scope and power.

## 3. The living standard (Article 10c)

Measured by real access, not by income or by any country's consumption:

- **Level 1, essential security:** a healthy home with water, sanitation, electricity, heating or cooling and clean cooking; nutritious food; health care; education; essential energy; Internet access; basic mobility. No one may lack these.
- **Level 2, comfortable life:** a comfortable home with a realistic path to owning it; varied and enjoyable meals; high-quality health care including prevention, dental care and medicines; free education for life; reliable car access, owned or shared, and good public transport; savings and an emergency reserve; holidays and leisure, including one holiday abroad a year; freedom in how to live, work, study and travel.

Starting physical values (research, revised every five years): about 27 m² of living space per person; about 2,470 kcal a day; about 131 health workers per 10,000 people; about 40 GJ of final energy per person; full education; digital access. The essential level starts at about 15 m², 2,100 kcal, 44.5 health workers and 15.3 GJ. Level 1 has priority whenever goods are short.

## 4. The economy (Part III)

**4.1 Allocation.** Each year the Allocation Authority directs new capacity (energy, health workers, homes, schools, infrastructure, goods) to regions by measured needs and population, through a public formula (10, 10a). Every region's gap to the essential level is closed before capacity goes to comfortable levels. Health need is weighted by age and disease burden together. Essential infrastructure (water, sanitation, clean cooking, electricity, basic health care, schooling) comes before any expansion of non-essential consumption (10e): repair before construction, local solutions first, local participation, training and maintenance funded for the whole life of a service, independent household-level monitoring before a programme starts, and delivery judged by working services rather than spending.

**4.2 The D + Q + C framework** (10d, T-8). From the first day:

- **D — rights in kind.** Essentials are guaranteed to every person independently of salary: suitable housing, health care, nutritious food, education and essential energy. Existing stocks are inventoried, redistributed, repaired and renovated before new production.
- **Q — fair shares of what is scarce.** A good is shared by published quotas, according to legitimate needs and population, only while its sustainable supply cannot meet demand. Quotas relax automatically as supply grows and are reviewed every year; none is permanent. Safeguards: adjustment for household size, age, climate, disability and medical need; no trade in allocation rights; transparency; automated routine administration with accessible alternatives; privacy; appeal; the right to decline or exchange without a speculative market.
- **C — salaries converge.** Regional salary scales rise toward the full scale as local capacity for services grows (research target about 20 years). Salaries follow the supply of needed goods, never total production; regional differences are temporary and never affect rights.

**4.3 Production** (10b, 11). Ordinary goods follow revealed demand: sales and waiting-list orders drive monthly production plans, with forecasts and modest stocks. No more is produced than is needed; each region keeps studied, rotated reserves of at least three months of storable essentials and emergency capacity for what cannot be stored. Production is located where people are available; nobody is relocated by force.

**4.4 Prices** (13). Prices of essentials are fixed. A price is the embodied hours of work at a fixed hour price (research value 146.4 U per hour) plus a physical resource surcharge, so that energy and materials are not under-priced. Each person has an essential energy allowance (research value about 5,000 kWh a year at the start) at the hours price; use above it is surcharged in steps; energy for food is never surcharged. Energy, travel and data are always metered per unit, never flat-rate (19).

**4.5 Money** (12, 12a, 17, 17a, 17b). One currency, the U, entirely digital and online; there is no cash and no offline payment device. Everyday payments are private below published limits; accounts can be frozen and data opened only by a court, for a named person and a stated offence; payment data never score anyone. The base salary is 5,000 U a month; multipliers for experience, knowledge and job complexity rise to at most 5×. Personal money is capped at 1.5 years of base salary, with protected accounts for a first home (150,000 U), retirement (120,000 U) and time (paid leave of at most two weeks a year, banked up to a three-month sabbatical). Money above the cap, including large inheritances, goes to the One Earth Fund. A Monetary Authority adjusts the cap each year by published rule, within ±10%, never by discretion. Zero-interest credit for a home is repaid from salary. Pay growth tracks productivity.

**4.6 Work** (7, 14, 14a, 14b, 15, T-10). The standard week is 30 hours, reached in each region from a measured starting week of at most 40 hours, never more than before joining, that falls each year with measured productivity at unchanged pay (T-10). Every adult able to work has the right and the duty to contribute, with free choice of occupation; illness, disability, care, study and retirement exempt without loss of income. Hard and unwanted jobs carry hardship steps, shorter hours and a duty to make hard work less hard. Retirement income is set by need, for life, including one holiday a year. Children receive an allowance; health care and education are free throughout life.

**4.7 Enterprises and investment** (16, 16a). Enterprises are run by their workers and communities; an owner's income is a capped salary; distributable surplus goes to the region, then to One Earth. Investment puts maintenance first, then needs, then hours-saving machines ranked by hours saved; savings shorten the week. Automation serves well-being (proposed 14c: a guaranteed income funded by the community share of automated production, with rewards for work still needed, and a skills reserve).

**4.8 Homes** (18, 18b, T-2). A home costs between 100,000 and 500,000 U by floor area and location; each adult and each family may own at most two. Buyers save at least half their salary or use zero-interest credit. Desirable homes are allocated by need with an open lottery share of 10% (at most 20% by regional choice); a must-live-in rule keeps homes occupied; an inherited home beyond the heir's first returns to the regional pool. Adults without a salary have guaranteed public housing. Second-hand goods are sold on a public platform at the fixed price minus wear, without bidding.

**4.9 Trade with non-members** (12b): a One Earth Trade Office imports in U at fixed prices, essentials first, with a five-year balance and six months of essential imports in reserve; the U is never traded on markets.

**4.10 No public debt** (20); **within Earth's limits** (21): energy, materials, land and emissions budgets bind every plan.

## 5. Institutions (Part IV and V)

- **Separation of powers** (22, 22a): law, executive, allocation, money, armed force, measuring needs, auditing, science and judging are held by different bodies, chosen by different methods; no person serves in two.
- **Assembly of the Peoples** (23): 1,000 members elected directly every five years; seats by the square root of population, no region above 5%.
- **Council of Regions** (24): one region, one vote. Laws need both chambers; amendments need two-thirds of the Assembly, 65% of regions with 65% of the population, and a world referendum won in a majority of regions (42, 42b).
- **Allocation Authority** (26, 26a): nine members, one term, no power to favour; the formula depends only on needs and population; formula-versus-actual published yearly; discretionary allocations at most 2%.
- **Statistical Office, Audit Authority, Scientific Council** (28, 29, 3a): independent, with budgets fixed by rule.
- **Court of One Earth** (32, 32a): 21 judges, one 12-year term, a third renewed every four years; every person may bring a case; rulings bind.
- **Citizens' assemblies by lot** (32b): a permanent Citizens' Council and large review assemblies for formula changes, amendments, long emergencies and deployments.
- **Safeguards** (33–39a): a public allocation register, direct digital delivery and physical verification, community monitoring and whistleblower protection, yearly random audits, data less than a year old, correction without punishing people, a built-in test of the allocation system (review above 25% leakage, reform above 45%), and a Democracy Monitor with automatic brakes.
- **Peace Force** (5a–5c) and a civilian **Mediation Service** (10f): the only armed body, capped at a million members and 0.15% of world output, mixed by rule, deployed only with a Court ruling and double majority, weapons under three keys; mediation, early warning and dispute resolution are unarmed, supervised by lot-chosen panels, and never tools of surveillance.
- **The protected core** (42a): rights, elections, term limits, the separation of powers, the Force's limits, court-order protection of money and data, emergency limits, the right to leave, and the equality of regions cannot be amended away.

## 6. Peace and conflict (Articles 4c, 5, 10e.6, 10f)

UNIRA removes some causes of war (scarcity, exclusion from essentials, debt, arms) and keeps a single, constrained force to enforce rights and rulings. It does not claim that prosperity brings peace: territorial, identity and power conflicts need political means. Essential services are delivered in a way that does not increase tensions, are never withheld from a region judged unstable, and are accompanied by mediation, early warning and cooperation between communities, independently monitored for whether they reduce or increase conflict risk. For ten years after a conflict ends, a region may ask for priority support for a lasting peace, allocated by the same needs-based formula (10f.6); institutional reform in weak or distrustful settings is introduced in agreed stages that never delay rights, services or safeguards (T-9). During the transition a member keeps its existing forces for defence only, under the Inspector-General and never inside the federation, until the Court finds that its security no longer depends on them; they are then dissolved within five years (T-5).

## 7. Transition (Transitional provisions T-1 to T-10)

The phased plan, with objectives, measured triggers and the dates the models give, is in `UNIRA_TRANSITION_PLAN.md`. Regions join voluntarily by referendum and may leave the same way. On joining: wages move onto the U scale; essential prices are set from embodied work; savings convert at basket parity, with the cap phased in over ten years; homes are never seized and mortgages become zero-interest credit; enterprise owners are compensated in U under the cap; public debts of members are cancelled, with ordinary savers' bonds protected; armed forces serve defence only until the region's security no longer depends on them, then are dissolved within five years with work, training and income for every member (T-5); safeguards and the Inspector-General are in place before any force is built; the D + Q + C framework applies from the first day; the starting week is measured and the path to 30 hours is published (T-10).

## 9. How UNIRA differs from what exists

| | Market economies | Planned economies of the 20th century | UNIRA |
|---|---|---|---|
| Allocation of new capacity | purchasing power | central plan, political priorities | public formula: needs × population, essential first |
| Prices | market, volatile | fixed, with chronic shortage | fixed hours-based prices with a physical surcharge, balanced by planned supply, buffer stocks, quotas only where scarce, and incomes that cannot exceed available goods |
| Money | credit-created, accumulating | state-issued, soft | digital U, capped per person, surplus to a common fund |
| Pay | unbounded | compressed, informal privilege | 1×–5×, visible and earned |
| Information | prices | reports to the centre | measured needs, open models, orders and waiting lists, audits, community monitoring |
| Power | capital and states | the party | separated bodies, lot-chosen assemblies, automatic brakes, a protected core, the right to leave |
| Force | national armies | national armies | one capped, mixed, many-keyed force |

The comparison with planned economies is the one critics will make first. UNIRA's answer is institutional (open data, quotas that expire, incomes tied to supply, exit rights). Whether that answer holds in practice is untested (section 11).

## 11. Known gaps and open problems

These are the questions the research cannot answer with models. They are the reason UNIRA is a proposal, not a plan.

1. **Behaviour.** No UNIRA economy has existed. Take-up of work from the labour reserve at the starting week (T-10), saving under a cap, effort under 1×–5× pay, honesty in reporting needs, and the response to quotas are all assumptions (`v82/BEHAVIOUR_REGISTER.md`). Only a pilot can test them.
2. **The knowledge problem.** Planning production for billions of people and millions of products from measured needs, orders and forecasts is tractable in parts (V7.8) and unproven as a whole. Quality, novelty and local preference are where plans have failed before.
3. **Fixed prices and shortage.** Every economy that froze prices for long saw queues and informal markets. UNIRA's answer (supply planned to needs, buffer stocks, incomes tied to available goods, quotas that expire) is a design, not a demonstration.
4. **Incentives for innovation** under capped rewards and no private capital: evidence supports open research and prizes, but at the scale of a world economy it is untested.
5. **The political path.** Nothing in the research shows how a majority of regions would choose to join, or how the first members coexist with a non-member world that keeps armies and markets. The transition rules describe conditions, not a route. Since 8 November 2026, T-5 lets a first member keep its defence until its security no longer depends on it, which removes one reason to refuse; but the thresholds (federation size, Peace Force strength) are unstudied, and the economic chapters have not been checked for a member trading with a market world.
6. **Sovereignty and consent.** Dissolving national armed forces and pooling resources is a premise. Many people would experience it as a loss, whatever the gains in living standards; the research does not weigh that.
7. **Institutions at world scale.** Lot-chosen assemblies, automatic brakes, a 21-judge court and a mixed force are drawn from documented precedents at national scale. No evidence exists for them at world scale.
8. **Culture and plurality.** Subsidiarity protects regional ways of life, but one allocation formula, one currency and one standard of living still embody choices that not every culture shares (what counts as comfortable, how to weigh needs). The Scientific Council's thresholds are a technocratic answer to a political question.
9. **Data and privacy at scale.** Needs-based allocation requires measurement of every household. The constitution limits what may be collected and how; the risk of misuse remains, and depends on institutions holding.
10. **Measurement.** Regional and national averages hide inequality within countries; floor-area and workforce figures are partly modelled; input–output coefficients are euro-based proxies for physical structure.
11. **Conflict.** Fair distribution and fair institutions are associated with less war; causation is not established, and wars between states are outside the models.
12. **Automation.** Model B (guaranteed income plus rewards for work) is a proposal with an illustrative simulation, not a tested design.
13. **What "comfortable" health care is.** The Level 2 health target (131 doctors, nurses and midwives per 10,000) is the EU's staff density, not a measured outcome. It decides the date more than any other lever: 23 years at 131, 15 at 100, 8 at 80 (v66 levers). An outcome-based threshold (healthy life years, waiting times) and the share of need that community health workers and care workers can meet are open research questions; sources must be verified before either enters the paper.
14. **Methods of building and training at scale.** Factory-built homes, household-built extensions and training with theory online and practice in rotation are modelled from verified evidence at project or programme scale (modular construction savings, mutual self-help housing, simulation replacing half of clinical hours, blended learning). None has been run at the scale of a region's whole housing or training system; the model treats them as multipliers, not as demonstrated capacity.

## 12. How UNIRA could be tested

- **A pilot** (`v80/pilot/`): a 30-hour, full-employment town inside today's law, with pre-registered predictions and failure criteria on employment take-up, productivity, hoarding, satisfaction and cost.
- **Monitoring built into the design**: household-level delivery monitoring (10e.4), conflict-effect monitoring (10e.6), the allocation-system test (39), the democracy health check (39a). Each has a published trigger that forces review or reform.
- **Falsification tests** stated in the paper: employment cannot exceed about 85%; demand at fixed prices exceeds reserves; training cannot grow beyond 1.5×; clean-energy build stays below the 2025 record; construction cannot add the required floor area; a reformed market matches the outcomes; leakage cannot be kept below 45% with data under five years old.
- **Independent review**: the review package in `v77/review/` and the self-audit of 4 November 2026.
