# Proposed constitution change: a self-correcting rule against shortages at fixed prices

**Status (8 November 2026): proposed, awaiting the author's decision.** Drafted in answer to the author's question "how can we solve the gaps": it is the design's answer to the behaviour gap (fixed prices with planned supply failing into queues and informal markets) and to attacks E1 and E2 of the review brief (`v77/review/REVIEW_BRIEF.md` §8). It adds no new instrument; it puts the existing ones (reserves, imports, quotas, the price rule, the allocation test) into a loop with fixed steps and published triggers, so that failure is detected and corrected instead of denied.

## What the evidence supports

Every economy that froze prices for long saw queues, rationing and informal markets (Kornai, *Economics of Shortage*, 1980; design rules, "Specific risks of rules 8–9"). The fixed price held; availability did not. UNIRA's instruments exist in the text, but nothing says when each is used, in what order, and what happens when they fail. A design that admits failure and corrects it by rule is more believable to reviewers, and safer for people, than one that assumes it will not fail.

## Proposal — Article 13, new point 9 "Shortage correction"

> **9. Shortage correction.** For every good with a fixed price, each region publishes three indicators every month: the waiting time for the good, the share of orders unfilled after three months, and the volume of informal exchange at prices above the fixed price. For each indicator a threshold is set by law and published. When any indicator stays above its threshold for three months, the following steps are taken in order, each within three months and each published with its data:
>    1. production is reallocated to the good and the regional reserve (T-8.7) is released;
>    2. the Trade Office imports the good (Article 12b), needs first;
>    3. a temporary quota by need is set (Article 10d), with a public end date;
>    4. the good's price is reviewed under Articles 13.2 to 13.7, including its resource surcharge, because a persistent shortage at a fixed price means the price no longer reflects the hours and resources the good embodies;
>    5. if the indicators stay above threshold for three years in all, the region may change the method of allocation for that good under Article 39, and the Scientific Council reports why the fixed price failed.
> Essential goods for the needs of Article 6 are never left under step 3 for more than one year without step 4.

## Why each step

- **Three indicators, not one.** Waiting time catches shortage in services, unfilled orders in goods, informal prices the moment a shadow market forms. Thresholds are parameters set by law (T-8.9), not constitutional numbers.
- **The order follows cost and reversibility.** Reserves and reallocation first (cheap, reversible), imports second (cost in foreign currency), quotas third (a cost to people, time-limited by 10d), a price review fourth (the step most against the grain of fixed prices, hence last but one), a change of method last (the Article 39 test, already in the text).
- **Step 4 is the honest step.** A price that is fixed "from embodied work" can be wrong, either because the work changed or because the surcharge undervalues a scarce resource. Reviewing it under the price rule is not a market price; it is a correction of the calculation.
- **Step 5 makes the system falsifiable in practice**, good by good, rather than only in a review every five years.

## Not proposed

- No floating prices and no bidding. Step 4 recalculates the fixed price under the existing rule; it does not let demand set it.
- No permanent quotas: 10d already forbids them, and step 3 carries an end date.
- No new body: the indicators are published in the Register (26a, 33); the Monetary Authority (17b) and the Scientific Council (3a) already exist.

## Consistency notes

- **Article 10d** (quotas only for genuinely scarce goods, relaxing automatically): step 3 is such a quota, with the end date 10d requires.
- **Article 12b** (Trade Office, needs first): step 2 uses it as written.
- **Article 13.2–13.7** (price from embodied hours and resources): step 4 is a review under these points, not an exception to them.
- **Article 17b** (Monetary Authority prevents purchasing power exceeding supply): the indicators give it the signal it needs good by good.
- **Article 39** (the allocation test): step 5 is its application to one good.
- **T-8.7** (reserves): step 1 draws on them, and the reserve rule says they are rotated and rebuilt.
- **Article 9** (rights cannot be reduced): the last sentence protects essential goods from a long quota without a price review.
- **Article 42a (protected core):** not touched.
