# UNIRA — The transition plan

**Status (6 November 2026): preliminary, not peer reviewed.** Every date below comes from this project's models on official data; the ranges are from the joint-uncertainty runs (`validation/uncertainty.py`). The dates are what the models give under their stated conditions, not promises. The constitution (`v75/constitution/`) holds the rules; this document orders them in time.

## 1. Why a transition plan

UNIRA's objectives (a comfortable life for everyone, a 30-hour week, fixed prices, no war, a world within ecological limits) cannot all be reached on the day a region joins. Homes, clinics, power plants and trained people take years to build; a money system needs time to settle; institutions need time to prove themselves. The research gives, for each objective, the time it takes and what it depends on. A plan that says so is more credible than one that promises everything at once, and it tells each region what to expect and when.

The plan has four phases. **Phases end on measured triggers, not on dates.** The dates are the models' central estimates, with ranges, so that progress can be checked against them.

## 2. What a stable UNIRA economy is

The end of the transition is defined by conditions that the constitution's own instruments can measure. The economy is stable when all of these hold for five consecutive years in every member region:

| # | Condition | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Every person has Level 1 (essential security): a healthy home with water, sanitation, electricity and clean cooking; food; health care; education; essential energy; connectivity; basic mobility | Household-level delivery monitoring (Article 10e.4), yearly report (T-8.6) |
| 2 | No essential good is under quota; shortage indicators (waiting times, unfilled orders) at zero | Quotas (T-8.3), allocation-system test (Article 39) |
| 3 | Unspent money stays between about 10 and 14 months of consumption; the cap moves by rule within ±10% a year | Monetary Authority (Article 17b); research basis V6.4, V7.9 |
| 4 | Average pay grows no faster than productivity; prices of essentials fixed | Articles 13 and 14.4 |
| 5 | Every region holds at least three months of essential goods in reserve | T-8.7 |
| 6 | Allocation leakage well below the 45% failure level, with data under a year old | Independent audits (Article 36), open publication (26a); research basis V6.9 |
| 7 | Everyone able to work is at work; the standard week is at 30 hours or on its published path (T-10, if adopted) | Article 7, T-10, yearly report |
| 8 | The democracy health check and the Court function as designed | Articles 32, 36, 39a |
| 9 | No armed conflict in the federation for ten years; the Mediation Service in place | Articles 5, 10e.6, 10f |
| 10 | World final energy at or below its planned peak and falling; the clean share rising | Paper §6; Article 11 |

Until all ten hold, the region is in transition and says so.

## 3. The four phases

### Phase 0 — Before joining (observer stage, Article 40)

- A **pilot** inside today's law: a town of about 20,000 people with a shorter week, guaranteed work and household delivery monitoring, with pre-registered predictions and failure criteria (`v80/pilot/`). The only step that replaces assumptions about behaviour with data.
- **Safeguards before power** (T-7): courts, audits, the Inspector-General and the allocation rules exist before any allocation or force.
- **Inventory** of what the region has (homes, clinics, networks, workers, reserves) so that redistribution and repair come before new production (T-8.2).
- **The starting week is measured** (T-10, proposed): the shortest week the region's existing labour reserve can cover without loss of output. Research values: 33–40 hours in Europe, 35 in France (V9.2).

*Trigger to Phase 1:* referendum to join (Article 40); safeguards certified in place.

### Phase 1 — Secure (from day one until every region has Level 1 in the plan-built domains)

What happens from the first day:
- Rights and the essential guarantees apply at once (Article 10d.2); where something must first be built, the right is a published priority with a deadline (T-8.2).
- **Decent for everyone first:** new capacity closes every region's gap to the essential level before any comfortable gap (Article 10a; V7.9).
- **Faculties and trainers first** in health: the training-capacity cap, not course length, decides the date of care for all (V6.6 levers).
- Wages move onto the U scale at the full base salary, whatever the starting week; prices of essentials set from embodied work; savings convert at basket parity; the money cap phases in over ten years (T-1).
- Armed forces are dissolved within five years of full membership, every member offered work, training and full income (T-5); safeguards govern any Peace Force (5a–5c).
- The essential-infrastructure programme (water, sanitation, clean cooking) runs at up to three times the best observed delivery pace, with local participation (V8.8).
- Paced institutional reform where institutions are weak (T-9); ten years of support after any conflict ends (10f.6).

*Trigger to Phase 2:* every region of the federation at the essential level in energy, health staffing and housing; household monitoring confirms delivery, not just construction.

### Phase 2 — Build (until every region has Level 2 energy and health, and housing is half closed)

- Clean energy built at 1.2–1.8 TW a year; rich regions cut final energy use 3–4% a year so that convergence and decarbonisation do not compete for the same build (paper §6).
- Health training expanded for 15–30 years (V6.6); home building grows toward 6–7 billion m² a year with low-carbon materials (paper §5).
- Quotas on scarce goods relax and end as supply grows (T-8.3); the money cap is fully in place by year 10 (T-1).
- The working week falls each year by measured productivity growth, pay unchanged (Article 13.3; T-10): from 35 hours, 30 hours comes in about 28 years at France's 2015–19 productivity trend, 16 years at 1% a year, 8 years at 2% a year, and effectively never at the 2015–23 trend (V9.2). Nothing is assumed about automation; the rule follows the measurement.

*Trigger to Phase 3:* every region at comfortable energy and health staffing; the housing gap at least half closed; no essential good under quota.

### Phase 3 — Converge (until comfortable housing everywhere and local services converge)

- Housing finishes: the comfortable standard reaches the last regions after year 30 in most samples.
- **Local services decide the long run.** Care, teaching, transport, shops, repair and leisure cannot be shipped; they are the largest gap between regions. Salaries reach the full scale as local capacity grows (T-8.4). The models put 90% of today's French level of services in the poorest regions at about year 50–69 depending on growth (V8.5).
- The 30-hour week is reached in every region where productivity has delivered it; where it has not, the yearly report says so (T-10.6).
- A comfortable life for 90% of humanity: year 72 in the accelerated scenario while today's conflicts continue; years 47 / 54 / 59 for 90 / 95 / 99% if conflicts end (V8.9). Conflict is the single largest barrier to this phase, not resources.

*Trigger to Phase 4:* every region at Level 2 in all plan-built domains; local services at or above 90% of the reference level; the ten stability conditions of section 2 holding.

### Phase 4 — Stable

The ten conditions of section 2 hold for five consecutive years in every region. From then on productivity gains go to time and to the guaranteed income of Article 14c, if adopted; standards are revised every five years on evidence (Article 10c); the transition provisions T-1 to T-10 lapse.

## 5. What would stop the clock

The models identify five things that delay every phase; each has a published trigger that forces review.

1. **Armed conflict.** From about year 10, most people still short of Level 1 live in conflict-affected states (V8.9). Prevention through fair institutions is the answer the research supports (V9.1); it is slow and uncertain.
2. **Leakage of allocations** above about 45%, or data older than about five years: needs-first allocation then does no better than a cooperative market (V6.9; Article 39 test).
3. **Take-up of work** below the starting-week requirement: shorter hours alone create no jobs; childcare, training and matching must exist (V6.2; T-10.5).
4. **Shortages at fixed prices:** demand above reserves, which the Monetary Authority and quotas must catch (V6.4; Articles 17b, 10d).
5. **Build rates** below the plan: clean energy under 1.2 TW a year, home building under 4–7 billion m² a year, health training without new faculties (paper §5–6).

## 6. What this plan is not

- It is not a forecast. The dates assume the six conditions of the paper (§10), institutions that work as designed, and behaviour that no UNIRA economy has yet shown.
- The models have no feedback between domains: a housing shortfall does not slow health in the model, though it would in life. The ranges are therefore probably too narrow.
- Nothing here is peer reviewed. The review questions (`v77/review/REVIEW_QUESTIONS.md` §12) ask reviewers which triggers are unmeasurable and which phase boundary is most likely to be crossed on paper only.
