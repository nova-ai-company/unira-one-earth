# UNIRA: Migration and displacement evidence review

**Claim tested.** In UNIRA there are no wars, and everyone has guaranteed income, housing and health, with equal living standards across regions. The author says refugees and economic migration would therefore largely disappear, and each region would control its own borders and visas.

**Method.** Every number below was quoted from source text I fetched (`verified_in_fetched_text = yes` in `evidence.csv`), except where it is marked otherwise. unhcr.org and internal-displacement.org blocked automated fetches with 403 errors or a captcha. For those two I used verbatim PDF mirrors of the official reports instead (asile.ch for UNHCR, the Colombian Unidad para las Víctimas for IDMC); the URLs are in `evidence.csv`. Figures labelled "assumption" are my own estimates and are not sourced.

## 1. Forced displacement today (UNHCR Global Trends 2025, published June 2026)

- At end-2025, **117.8 m** people were forcibly displaced, about 1 in 70 people worldwide. That is down from 123.2 m at end-2024, the first fall in a decade. The breakdown:
  - **41.6 m refugees**: 35.6 m under UNHCR's mandate, which includes 7.2 m "other in need of international protection", mostly Venezuelans. The other 6 m are Palestine refugees under UNRWA's mandate.
  - **9 m asylum-seekers.**
  - **68.7 m conflict IDPs** (internally displaced people), who make up 58% of the total.
- **Causes:** UNHCR's definition of "forcibly displaced" itself names the causes: persecution, conflict, violence, human rights violations, and events seriously disturbing public order. So this whole stock is caused by exactly the things UNIRA says it removes.
- **Where they live:** 65% are hosted in neighbouring countries and 68% in low- and middle-income countries (the 2024 figures were 67% and 73%). 70% are in protracted exile, meaning five years or more.
- **New flows:** 5.4 m people crossed borders in 2025. In 2024 there were 20.1 m new internal displacements caused by conflict.

## 2. Disaster displacement (IDMC GRID 2025)

- **45.8 m** new disaster displacements in 2024, the highest since records began in 2008. The 15-year average is **24 m per year**.
- **Weather-related: 45.5 m, or 99.3%.** Storms caused 25.2 m and floods 19.1 m. Many of these were short pre-emptive evacuations (8.3 m explicitly).
- **9.8 m** people were still displaced by disasters at end-2024. This group is **not** counted in UNHCR's total.
- A search summary says 2025 had 29.9 m disaster displacements (GRID 2026). I did not fetch that report, so this figure is unverified.
- **Climate projection:** the World Bank's Groundswell report (2021) projects up to **216 m** internal climate migrants by 2050. It says strong climate and development action could cut this by up to 80%, which still leaves about **43 m**.

## 3. Economic and voluntary migration

- **Size:** UN DESA counts **281 m** international migrants in 2020, less than 4% of the world's population. Refugees and asylum-seekers are **12%** of that, so roughly 88% of migrants are not forced.
  - The 2024 figure of 304 m (3.7%) comes from secondary sources and is unverified.
  - The World Bank's WDR 2023 uses a narrower count: 184 m people living outside their country of nationality.
- **Income gaps drive moves.** The same worker earns about 2.7 times more in the US than at home in the median country, about 3.5 times for the Philippines and more than 7 times for Haiti (Clemens, Montenegro & Pritchett 2008). WDR 2023 says: "Migration is a response to global imbalances, such as large welfare differences."
- **The migration hump.** Clemens (2014, 2020) finds that emigration *rises* as a country develops and starts to fall only after about **PPP$10k** of GDP per head. Before that turning point, doubling GDP per head goes with **+35%** emigration to all destinations and **+74%** to rich countries.
  - Share of the population living abroad: about 4% at PPP$1k, about 11% at PPP$10k.
  - At PPP$40k, emigration is "no longer statistically distinguishable" from the level in the poorest countries. So even rich societies keep a few percent of their population living abroad.

## 4. Do people go home when wars end?

- **Mozambique:** after the October 1992 peace accord, about **1.7 m** refugees had been repatriated by June 1995. UNHCR's yearbook records 604,387 in 1993 and 804,376 in 1994. By mid-1994, **75%** of the 3.7 m IDPs had been resettled.
- **Syria:** after the Assad government fell in December 2024, more than 500k refugees returned by mid-May 2025, along with 1.2 m IDPs. 1.3 m refugees returned during 2025 as a whole.
- **Worldwide:** 14.7 m people returned in 2025 (4.4 m refugees and 10.3 m IDPs), the second-highest year for refugee returns since 1965.
- **Caveat:** UNHCR says most of these returns happened "under adverse circumstances", including forced returns of Afghans from neighbouring countries. Returns are not automatic: long exile, children born abroad and destroyed property keep some people where they are.

## Verdict for UNIRA

1. **Refugees: largely supported, if UNIRA's premises hold.** UNHCR's own definition traces essentially all 117.8 m forcibly displaced people to conflict, persecution, violence and human rights abuse. If those causes are gone, new refugee flows go to about zero, and history (Mozambique, Syria) shows large returns soon after peace. Three caveats:
   - Some long-term exiles will stay where they are, and should be treated as integrated residents rather than refugees.
   - The 7.2 m "other in need of international protection" were also pushed out by economic collapse, which UNIRA's guarantees address separately.
   - UNIRA should still keep a protection procedure as a fallback.
2. **Disaster and climate displacement: not removed.** In 2024, disasters caused **more than twice as many** new displacements as conflict (45.8 m against 20.1 m), and 99% were weather-related. In UNIRA this becomes mostly a matter of short evacuations and internal relocation. That works only if regions guarantee rehousing and accept relocations from neighbouring regions when an area becomes uninhabitable. Groundswell projects 43–216 m internal climate migrants by 2050.
3. **Economic migration: "largely disappears" overstates it.** Converging living standards removes the wage-gap motive (a place premium of 2.7 times or more), which is the main driver. But rich societies still have a few percent of their people living abroad, for family, study, careers and preference. The migration hump also means that *while* poorer regions catch up, emigration pressure **rises** before it falls. The accurate wording: distress migration disappears, and voluntary mobility stays at a stable few percent.
4. **Border control:** this works only if visas cannot be used to keep out disaster relocations or people seeking protection. Otherwise a region's "control" collides with the residual flows described in points 2 and 3. Suggested rule: free or easy mobility between regions as the default, with duties to admit people in emergencies.
